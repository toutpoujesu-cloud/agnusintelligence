AI Office and AI Board Governance Structure Under the EU AI Act (Articles 64–70)

Four interlocking governance bodies — the AI Office, the European AI Board, the Advisory Forum, and the Scientific Panel — plus the national competent authorities they coordinate: who they are, what they do, and how they divide responsibility for implementing the Act.

    Requirements

    What organizations must do

    • Check your national competent authority’s published single-point-of-contact route — required to be public since 2 August 2025.
    • For GPAI obligations, note the scientific panel is the body that may issue “qualified alerts” on systemic risk to the AI Office under Article 90, feeding directly into Article 55 obligations.
    • Do not treat the Board as an enforcement contact — route enforcement-relevant matters to the AI Office (GPAI) or the national market surveillance authority (high-risk systems).

    Common mistakes

    Treating the AI Board as an enforcement body is a common mistake — the Board’s tasks are advisory, coordination-based, and opinion-issuing, not directly executive. The Commission (via the AI Office) and national market surveillance authorities are the enforcement actors.

    Assuming there is a single "EU AI regulator" for all AI Act matters is a second mistake — GPAI supervision sits with the AI Office at Commission level, while high-risk AI system enforcement sits with national market surveillance authorities, per the jurisdictional rules AR-000280 covered in depth.

    Overlooking the Advisory Forum’s five permanent members — FRA, ENISA, CEN, CENELEC, ETSI — is a third mistake: their permanent status gives those bodies a standing seat in the advisory process, not an occasional input.

    Open questions

    • Whether Articles 64, 65, 66, 67, 68, or 70 are genuinely unamended by the Digital Omnibus. None carry an amendment disclaimer on their Service Desk pages (65–70 via full page fetch; 64 via URL-indexed snippet), but absence of a disclaimer is not confirmation.
    • Whether the Article 68(1) implementing act formally constituting the scientific panel has been adopted, and the panel’s membership and size determined. Not independently confirmed against a primary source on this pass.
    • Whether all Member States met the Article 70(2) deadline of 2 August 2025 to publish single-point-of-contact information. Not systematically verified against the Commission’s published list.
