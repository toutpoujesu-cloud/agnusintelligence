Market Surveillance and Enforcement Under the EU AI Act (Articles 74–81)

How national market surveillance authorities police the AI Act day to day: who has jurisdiction over which systems, the corrective-action procedure that follows a risk finding, and the Union safeguard procedure that resolves disagreements between Member States.

Key context
"Market surveillance authorities have significant powers: they can request documentation, conduct testing, order withdrawals, and impose provisional measures — all without prior court authorisation in urgent cases."
EU AI Act, Article 74
A customs inspector's stamp poised over a shipping manifest, evoking the market surveillance authority's power to halt a non-compliant product at the point of market access.

    Requirements

    What organizations must do

    • Identify the correct market surveillance authority for each system before an issue arises — an organisation with both Annex I sectoral systems and Annex III systems may face genuinely different regulators for different products.
    • Treat any market surveillance evaluation as time-pressured from the outset — 15 working days is the outer limit for corrective action, not a guaranteed grace period.
    • Build Union-wide remediation capability rather than assuming a national finding stays contained to one jurisdiction — the corrective-action duty extends to every instance made available across the Union market.
    • Run an internal audit against Article 83's five formal non-compliance triggers as a distinct, lower-bar compliance check separate from substantive risk assessment.

    Common mistakes

    Assuming a single "AI regulator" per Member State handles all AI Act enforcement is a common mistake — Article 74's jurisdictional rules split authority by sector, deployer type, and Annex III category.

    Treating a market surveillance finding as final once corrective action is taken locally is a second mistake — the cross-notification duty and the Article 81 safeguard procedure mean a national measure can still be escalated or contested after initial local action.

    Conflating formal non-compliance under Article 83 with the risk-based procedure under Article 79 is a third mistake — a system can be procedurally non-compliant while presenting no actual risk, and the authority response differs accordingly.
