Singapore AI Governance: Model Governance Framework and Smart Nation Strategy

Singapore has built one of the world's most detailed voluntary AI governance frameworks — the Model AI Governance Framework — while maintaining a pro-innovation stance that avoids binding AI legislation. As the regional hub for AI in Southeast Asia, Singapore's governance approach influences the broader ASEAN region and serves as a practical model for business-facing AI governance documentation.

Singapore's approach: voluntary frameworks, sector sandboxes

Singapore's AI governance strategy is characterised by detailed, business-facing voluntary frameworks rather than binding legislation. The Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) and the Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC) have co-developed a suite of AI governance documentation that is among the most practically useful in the world — designed to be directly usable by organisations developing AI governance programmes.

Singapore's Smart Nation initiative, launched 2014 and evolved significantly since, positions AI as central to Singapore's economic and public service development. This creates a dual role for Singapore's AI governance apparatus: enabling AI adoption at scale while providing governance guardrails that maintain public trust and international credibility.

Singapore's National AI Strategy (NAIS 2.0, 2023) targets Singapore as a leader in AI governance internationally — not just in AI development. This means Singapore's frameworks are designed for export as well as domestic use, and Singapore has been active in ASEAN-level AI governance harmonisation efforts.

The Model AI Governance Framework

The Model AI Governance Framework (MGF), first published in 2019 and updated to version 2 in 2020, is the most comprehensive AI governance documentation produced by any government in the world to date. It covers internal governance structures and competencies, human involvement in AI decision-making, operations management, and stakeholder interaction and communication.

The MGF's version 2 introduces an implementation and self-assessment guide, making it one of the few governance frameworks that includes a structured methodology for organisations to assess their own compliance. Each recommendation in the MGF is accompanied by illustrative examples of implementation at different levels of sophistication — making it useful for organisations at varying stages of AI governance maturity.

Singapore also published Artificial Intelligence in Hiring (2019) and Artificial Intelligence in Credit (2021) sector-specific guidance, demonstrating how the MGF applies in high-stakes domains. These sector guides are directly relevant to organisations deploying AI in financial services and HR in Singapore.

The MGF does not have binding legal force. However, it is used as the reference framework for the PDPC's AI governance guidance, for MAS (Monetary Authority of Singapore) guidance on AI in financial services, and for the AI Verify Foundation's evaluation framework. Non-compliance with the MGF creates no direct regulatory liability, but it creates reputational and regulatory risk when an incident occurs and the organisation cannot demonstrate it had a structured governance approach.

PDPA and AI

The Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), amended significantly in 2021, governs AI processing of personal data in Singapore. The 2021 amendments added consent exceptions for legitimate interests and business improvement, which are broadly used by organisations training AI on operational data.

The PDPC has published specific AI governance guidance under the PDPA, covering: the use of personal data for AI training; automated decision-making affecting individuals; and data minimisation in AI deployments. Notably, the PDPC's guidance on automated decision-making is less restrictive than GDPR Article 22 — it does not create a right not to be subject to automated decisions, but requires meaningful explanation and a human review process for significant decisions.

For organisations with operations in both Singapore and the EU, the PDPA-GDPR intersection requires attention. PDPA consent exceptions (legitimate interests, business improvement) may not have equivalents under GDPR for the same data processing activities. Organisations should map PDPA legal bases to GDPR legal bases for AI processing activities that span both jurisdictions.

For operators: key implications

Voluntary frameworks create de facto standards. The MGF is not legally binding, but regulators (MAS, PDPC) reference it. An organisation that cannot demonstrate alignment with the MGF in a regulatory inquiry is at a disadvantage even without a binding compliance requirement.

AI Verify provides a testing mechanism. AI Verify, developed by IMDA, is an AI governance testing framework that allows organisations to test and document their AI systems against the MGF. AI Verify-certified systems provide evidence of governance for procurement and regulatory purposes.

PDPA legitimate interests is broader than GDPR. The PDPA's business improvement and legitimate interests exceptions give Singapore-based organisations more flexibility in processing personal data for AI training than EU GDPR. Organisations with cross-border operations cannot rely on PDPA exceptions for GDPR-governed data.
