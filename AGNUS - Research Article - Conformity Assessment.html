Conformity Assessment Under the EU AI Act

Which conformity assessment route applies depends on one variable: which Annex III category (or Annex I Section A product) the system falls under. Article 43 maps three distinct routes, each with its own notified-body involvement rule. Substantially modified systems must repeat the assessment.

Key context
"The conformity assessment is not a one-time gate. If a high-risk system undergoes substantial modification after market placement, the assessment must be repeated."
EU AI Act, Article 43(4)
Conformity assessment is the formal verification gate between development and market placement — which route a system takes determines whether a notified body is involved at all.
Contents

    Builds on AR-000163 (Risk Classification), AR-000188 (Technical Documentation), and AR-000240 (Provider Obligations) — none of their content is re-derived here. Article 43 determines which conformity assessment procedure applies and whether a notified body must be involved. Article 47 (EU declaration of conformity) and Article 48 (CE marking) follow from a completed assessment.

    Executive summary

    The three routes at a glance

    Route 1 — Article 43(1)
    Annex III point 1 — Biometrics
    Internal control (Annex VI) OR notified body (Annex VII)
    Notified body mandatory if no harmonised standards/common specs apply or were not fully applied. Market surveillance authority acts as notified body for law enforcement/immigration/asylum/Union body systems.
    Route 2 — Article 43(2)
    Annex III points 2–8
    Internal control only (Annex VI)
    No notified body involvement. Commission can extend notified body requirement to these categories via Article 43(6) delegated act.
    Route 3 — Article 43(3)
    Annex I Section A products
    Sectoral legislation procedure
    AI Act Chapter III Section 2 requirements are folded into the sectoral assessment. Annex VII points 4.3–4.6 also apply. Notified bodies under sectoral law can assess AI-specific requirements.

    Requirements

    What organizations must do

    • Determine the correct route before beginning the assessment — the Annex III point (or Annex I Section A status) drives the entire procedure.
    • For Annex III point 1 systems: assess whether harmonised standards or common specifications exist and were applied in full — this determines whether internal control is available or a notified body is mandatory.
    • For law enforcement/immigration/asylum/Union-body biometric systems: do not select a commercial notified body — the market surveillance authority under Article 74(8) or (9) acts as the notified body.
    • For continuously-learning systems: pre-determine and document anticipated changes in Annex IV point 2(f) at the time of the initial assessment — this is the only way to protect those changes from triggering a new assessment.
    • Build a change-monitoring process that flags whether any post-market modification meets the Article 3(23) substantial-modification test — and triggers the Article 43(4) reassessment if it does.

    Common mistakes

    Assuming all high-risk AI systems require a notified body is a common mistake — Annex III points 2–8 use internal control only, with no notified body, and that covers most Annex III use-case categories.

    Treating the continuously-learning exception as a post-hoc claim rather than a pre-planned design decision is a second mistake — the pre-determined changes must be documented at the time of the initial assessment, not after the change occurs.

    Running a separate AI Act conformity assessment for an Annex I Section A product rather than integrating the AI requirements into the existing sectoral assessment is a third mistake — Article 43(3) requires a single combined procedure.

    Not repeating the conformity assessment when a system is substantially modified because the system is not being redistributed is a fourth mistake — Article 43(4) requires reassessment “regardless of whether the modified system is intended to be further distributed or continues to be used by the current deployer.”

    Open questions

    • Whether the Digital Omnibus changed Article 43’s route structure, notified-body trigger conditions, or reassessment rule — Article 43 carries the Omnibus disclaimer; not confirmed on this research pass.
    • Whether any AI Act harmonised standard has now been cited in the Official Journal — as of AR-000142’s prior research pass, none had been; not re-verified on this pass. If one now exists, it changes the Route 1 internal-control calculation.
    • Whether the Commission has exercised its Article 43(6) power to extend the notified-body requirement to Annex III points 2–8 — no such delegated act was identified on this research pass.

    Frequently asked

    FAQ
    Can any Annex III points 2–8 system choose to involve a notified body voluntarily?
    Article 43(2) prescribes internal control only for these systems — it does not provide for voluntary notified-body involvement. The Commission can extend the notified-body requirement to these categories via Article 43(6) delegated act, but until such an act is adopted, internal control is the prescribed procedure.
    FAQ
    If a continuously-learning system evolves in an unexpected direction after deployment, must the provider repeat conformity assessment?
    Yes, if the change constitutes a substantial modification under Article 3(23) and was not pre-determined in the initial technical documentation. The continuously-learning exception covers only pre-determined changes documented under Annex IV point 2(f) at the time of the initial assessment.
    FAQ
    Has the Digital Omnibus changed Article 43’s conformity assessment routes?
    Confirmed amended, but the Service Desk has not yet been updated. The route structure described here is the pre-amendment version of 13 June 2024.
