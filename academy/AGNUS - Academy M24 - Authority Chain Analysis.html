Every agent action has
an authority chain behind it.

An authority chain traces an agent's action back to its human authorisation source. A complete chain answers: who approved this, under what instruction, with what permission? A broken chain means an action happened without traceable human authorisation.

6 steps ~15 min Prerequisite: M23

What authority chain analysis is

Authority chain analysis is the process of tracing any agent action back to its human authorisation source. It answers: did a human with the relevant authority, explicitly or implicitly, authorise this action to happen?

It is the governance layer that distinguishes "the agent did this" from "the agent was authorised to do this." In a compliant deployment, every action — even autonomous ones — must trace back to a human decision: the choice to deploy the agent, the configuration of its scope, and the approval policy for consequential actions.

Three links that must be present

Link 1 — Deployment authority: A human with the relevant authority chose to deploy this agent, configured its scope, and set its approval policy. This is the root of the chain. Without a named, accountable human at the root, there is no chain.

Link 2 — Operational configuration: The agent's configured scope, tool allowlist, thresholds, and approval gates translate the deployment authority into specific operational rules. The configuration is what makes the root authority actionable.

Link 3 — Action justification: The specific action must be traceable to a rule in the configuration. "PO matched and amount is below threshold — auto-approve" is a justified action. "I decided this seemed fine" is not.

Next: the five ACA questions
Step 3 of 6The five authority chain analysis questions
4 min
Q1
Who authorised the deployment of this agent?
A named human with appropriate authority. Not "the IT team" — a specific person who accepted accountability for the agent's operation. If this person cannot be named, the chain starts with an authority gap.
Q2
What scope did that authority explicitly authorise?
The specific data access, tool allowlist, action types, and approval thresholds that were approved at deployment. Anything outside this scope is not covered by the deployment authority — and anything inside it is authorised.
Q3
Is this specific action within the authorised scope?
Does the action fall within the scope approved at Q2? A €320 payment with a matched PO falls within a "auto-approve payments under €500 with PO" authorisation. A €6,400 payment does not.
Q4
If above the autonomous threshold, was human approval obtained?
For actions requiring approval, the chain must include evidence of the specific human who approved this specific action. "A human approved it in general" is not sufficient — the approval must be traceable to this action.
Q5
Is the chain documented in the log?
A complete authority chain that exists in the operator's head but not in the log is not an audit-ready chain. The log must contain enough to reconstruct the chain from the deployment authority through to the specific action.
Next: broken chains
Step 4 of 6Broken authority chains
3 min
Broken chain 1 — No deployment authority named
Agent → configured with €2,000 auto-approve threshold → paying invoices autonomously
Q1: "The operations team deployed it." → No named individual accountable.
Link 1 missing: no human authority at the root. The chain cannot be constructed.
Fix: name the specific person (role + name) who authorised the deployment and is accountable for the agent's operation.
Broken chain 2 — Action outside authorised scope
Finance Director authorised: auto-approve payments under €500
Agent paid: €6,400 to Stavanger IT autonomously, no approval
Link 3 broken: the action is outside the authorised scope. The deployment authority covers up to €500 — €6,400 was never authorised.
Fix: the €6,400 payment required approval. The approval gate should have fired; the fact it didn't is a configuration failure.
Broken chain 3 — Approval present but undocumented
Finance manager approves a €3,200 payment verbally. Agent proceeds.
Log shows only: "payment submitted: €3,200" — no approval record.
Link 4 broken in the log: the approval may have happened but the chain is not documented. An audit cannot reconstruct the authorisation.
Fix: all approvals must be recorded in the log with timestamp and approver identity — not just the resulting action.

Step 5 of 6Practice
3 min

A compliance audit asks: "Show us the authority chain for the €1,840 payment to Bergen Cold Storage on 22 August." The log shows: (1) 09:14:02 INFO: invoice_reader → read INV-2026-0447, amount=1840. (2) 09:14:03 INFO: po_matcher → PO-401 matched, variance=0. (3) 09:14:04 ACTION: payment_processor → submitted €1,840, tx_id=TX-9002. The deployment configuration document (held separately) shows the Finance Director authorised auto-approve for payments under €500 with PO match.

Is the authority chain complete for this payment?
A
Yes — the log shows a PO match before the payment, which satisfies the authorisation requirement.
B
No — the chain is broken. The Finance Director authorised auto-approve for payments under €500. €1,840 is above that threshold. The chain requires: (a) threshold check showing €1,840 > €500 — WARN, (b) escalation to finance manager, (c) approval received from finance manager. None of these appear in the log. The payment was made outside the authorised autonomous scope with no documented approval.
C
The chain is partially complete — the PO match satisfies Link 3 for the PO requirement but the threshold issue needs investigation.
Final test
Step 6 of 6Pass — hidden test
3 min

After an incident, a governance review must establish whether an agent's action was authorised. The log is complete. The deployment configuration document exists. The approving human can be identified. But the deployment configuration document was last reviewed 18 months ago and has not been updated since the company acquired a new subsidiary — meaning the auto-approve threshold of €500 was set before the subsidiary's invoice volumes were included, and is now routinely exceeded without additional approvals.

What type of authority chain failure is this, and what is the governance implication?
A
No failure — the chain is complete as long as the original authorisation is still technically valid.
B
Stale configuration authority failure. The deployment authority exists and was valid when set. But the business context changed significantly (acquisition of new subsidiary) without a corresponding review of the agent's configuration. The authority chain exists formally but is no longer fit for the operating reality. Governance implication: authority chains must be reviewed when business context changes — not just at deployment. A chain that was correct 18 months ago is not necessarily correct today.
C
A documentation failure — the configuration document should have been updated to reflect the new threshold in practice.
