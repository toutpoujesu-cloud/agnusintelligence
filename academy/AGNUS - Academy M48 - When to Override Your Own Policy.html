AGNUS Academy Level 7 · Govern M48 — When to Override Your Own Policy
Step 1 of 4 — Understand4 min

Closing out Level 7: a policy is a tool for judgment, not a replacement for it.

M47 built the case for concrete, checkable policy over vague appeals to "good judgment." This module completes that picture: even the best-written policy will occasionally meet a situation it didn't anticipate, and knowing when a justified exception applies is itself a governance skill.

Why this matters

Treating a policy as absolute in every circumstance, with zero capacity for a reasoned exception, eventually produces its own failures — a rule applied rigidly to a case its authors never considered. The skill isn't "follow policy" or "override policy" as a blanket rule; it's recognizing the narrow, real situations where an exception is genuinely justified, and being able to explain why.

Step 2 of 4 — See4 min

A case where the policy's letter and its purpose diverge

The policy (from M47's model)

"Any AI-generated numeric claim in a customer-facing document must cite a specific, checkable source before publication."

The situation

A support agent needs to tell a customer, during an active service outage, "we expect service to be restored within the hour" — an estimate from the live incident-response team, not from a static source document the policy's citation requirement was designed around.

What makes this a justified exception, not just an excuse

1The information genuinely can't satisfy the policy's literal form — there's no static "source document" for a live incident estimate — without this meaning the underlying claim is unverified.
2The claim still has a real basis (the incident-response team's assessment), even though it doesn't fit the citation format the policy anticipated.
3The exception is documented and explainable after the fact — this isn't "we skipped verification," it's "we verified via a different, equally legitimate method the policy didn't originally name."

Note what this is not: it's not "the citation requirement is inconvenient right now, so we'll skip it." It's recognizing that the policy's actual purpose — don't state unverified numbers — is still being honored, even though its literal mechanism doesn't fit this case.

Step 3 of 4 — Practice4 min

What distinguishes a justified policy exception from simply ignoring the policy?

AWhether the person making the exception is senior enough to not need a reason.
BWhether the policy's actual underlying purpose is still being honored through a different, legitimate mechanism, and the reasoning is documented and explainable afterward.
CWhether following the policy would have taken more time than skipping it.
DWhether anyone would notice the exception was made.
EAny deviation from written policy counts as equally justified, regardless of reasoning.
