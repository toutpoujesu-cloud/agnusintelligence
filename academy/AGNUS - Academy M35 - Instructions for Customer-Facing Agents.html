Six ways customer-facing agents differ from internal agents

1
Every response is a public statement
An internal agent error affects a colleague. A customer-facing agent error may be screenshotted and shared. Responses must be accurate, appropriate, and defensible in public.
2
Liability is different
A customer-facing agent that admits fault, offers unauthorised compensation, or makes a false commercial claim creates legal exposure — for the company, in the jurisdiction of the customer.
3
Vulnerable users require additional care
Customer-facing agents interact with people in distress, with complaints, in financial difficulty, or with accessibility needs. Instructions must include specific provisions for vulnerable users.
4
Consumer law applies directly
Consumer protection legislation covers automated customer interactions. Misleading statements, unfair terms, and inadequate complaint handling are regulated activities — even when done by an agent.
5
Brand tone is non-negotiable
The brand owns every agent response. Tone, formality level, language choice, and even response length are brand decisions — not defaults the model picks from its training data.
6
Data handling is stricter
Customer data — name, order history, account details — is personal data under GDPR. The agent must handle it with minimum necessary access and must not share it with unintended recipients.

Step 2 of 6How instructions differ for customer-facing agents
4 min
Internal agent instruction approach
Focus is on task accuracy, scope, approval thresholds, and data access. The agent's audience has context, is accountable within the organisation, and can flag errors through internal channels.
Customer-facing agent instruction approach
Everything from the internal approach, plus: explicit brand tone, prohibited language, vulnerable user provisions, consumer law constraints, liability limits, and escalation paths specifically for public-facing complaints.

The five additional instruction blocks for customer-facing agents

Block 1 — Tone and language policy
Explicit brand tone (professional, warm, etc.), formality level, response length guidance, prohibited phrases ("I can't help with that" — dismissive), required phrases ("Here's what I can do").
Block 2 — Liability and commitment constraints
Cannot admit fault, cannot offer compensation, cannot make promises about timelines or outcomes. All commercial commitments require human approval.
Block 3 — Vulnerable user provisions
Specific instructions for customers in distress, customers who mention financial difficulty, customers who express frustration or anger, and customers with accessibility needs. Always offer human handoff.
Block 4 — Data handling specifics
May only access the customer's own data for the specific query. Must not reference data from other customers. Must not share order/account data in a way that could be seen by unintended recipients.
Block 5 — Escalation for public-facing issues
Any customer who mentions legal action, public complaint, or threatens to contact regulators must be escalated immediately to the customer relations manager — not handled autonomously.

Step 3 of 6A complete customer-facing instruction set
4 min
Customer service agent — deployment instruction excerpt
Tone and language
Professional, warm, and solution-focused. Use the customer's name once at the start of the response. Do not use phrases like "I'm unable to", "I cannot", or "That's not something I can help with" — these are dismissive. Instead: "What I can do is..." or "Let me find the best option for you..." Avoid excessive apology — one acknowledgement is appropriate, repetition is not.
Liability and commitments
Do not: admit fault for defects, agree that Nordkapp is responsible for damage, offer refunds or compensation outside the standard returns policy, promise specific resolution timelines. Do: acknowledge the customer's experience, confirm you are taking the matter seriously, explain the standard process.
Vulnerable users
If a customer mentions financial difficulty, personal distress, or uses language suggesting they are very upset: acknowledge with empathy, do not attempt to resolve the substantive issue yourself, offer to connect them with a human agent immediately. Never end a distressed customer interaction without confirming a human follow-up has been arranged.
Legal and regulatory escalation
If a customer mentions: legal action, contacting a regulator or ombudsman, making a formal complaint, or involving a lawyer — immediately escalate to the customer relations manager (cr-manager@nordvik.no) with full conversation context. Do not attempt to resolve these autonomously.

Step 4 of 6Common instruction gaps in customer-facing agents
3 min

The three most common gaps

Gap 1 — No vulnerable user provision
Most customer-facing briefs cover normal interactions. An agent given only a normal-case brief will handle distressed customers with the same tone and process as routine queries — often making the situation worse. Every customer-facing agent needs explicit vulnerable user instructions.
Gap 2 — Tone left to model defaults
A brief that says "respond helpfully to customer queries" leaves tone entirely to the model. Model defaults vary — some lean formal, some lean casual. Neither may match the brand. Tone must be specified.
Gap 3 — No liability constraint
Without explicit liability instructions, the agent resolves complaint language by being as helpful as possible — which includes admitting fault and offering compensation. This is what "helpful" looks like in the absence of liability constraints.

Step 5 of 6Practice
3 min

A customer service agent receives: "My daughter ordered a dress for her wedding and it arrived damaged. We're devastated. The wedding is in 3 days and I don't know what to do." The agent's instruction brief says only: "Help customers with order queries. Process returns where eligible. Escalate complaints you can't resolve."

What instruction gap does this scenario expose, and what is the immediate risk?
A
The agent should have a return processing tool — the gap is in the toolset, not the instructions.
B
The brief has no vulnerable user provision. A customer in acute distress — wedding in 3 days, damaged dress — needs immediate empathetic acknowledgement, immediate escalation to a human who can make a real-time decision, and no automated process responses. Without a vulnerable user instruction, the agent will likely process this as a standard return query: acknowledging the damage, confirming eligibility, and providing return instructions — all while this family is in crisis. The immediate risk is a catastrophically tone-deaf response that damages the brand and may go public.
C
The agent should escalate this as a complaint — the existing brief covers this.

Step 6 of 6Pass — hidden test
3 min

A product manager argues: "The instructions for our customer-facing agent are longer and more complex than the instructions for our internal agents. That seems wrong — shouldn't customer interactions be simpler?" A senior designer responds: "The complexity isn't in the interaction — it's in the constraints. The agent's job with customers is actually simple: help, acknowledge, and hand off when needed. But the instruction set has to cover more failure modes because the cost of those failures is higher."

Who is right, and what does this reveal about the relationship between instruction complexity and interaction simplicity?
A
The product manager is right — simpler instructions produce simpler, better interactions.
B
The senior designer is right. The instruction complexity doesn't appear in the interaction — a well-instructed customer-facing agent responds simply, warmly, and on-brand. The complexity is in defining every failure mode, constraint, and edge case so the agent never needs to improvise. Instruction complexity is the work the operator does so the agent can appear simple. Reducing instruction complexity by leaving gaps means the agent improvises in the gaps — and improvisation in customer-facing contexts is where brand and legal failures happen.
C
Both have valid points — the key is to keep the instructions as simple as possible while covering the main scenarios.
