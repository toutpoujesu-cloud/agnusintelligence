Module 32 of 49 · Level 5 · Verify

AI can draft legal language.
Only lawyers can take responsibility for it.

AI agents are increasingly capable of drafting contracts, NDAs, and legal clauses that look professionally competent. The risk is not that the output looks bad — it's that it looks good while containing legal errors that only a qualified lawyer would catch. Approval boundaries for legal drafts define when and how qualified review is required.

6 steps ~15 min Prerequisite: M31

The problem with AI-drafted legal language

A well-structured NDA with plausible commercial terms generated by an AI agent may contain jurisdiction errors, missing statutory requirements, unenforceable clauses, or outdated references to superseded legislation. These errors are invisible to a non-lawyer reading the document. The document reads as professional and complete — and then fails to protect the party it was meant to protect.

Approval boundaries for legal drafts answer the question: which AI-generated legal outputs require qualified lawyer review, and which can be used directly? The answer is never "none" — but the review depth should be calibrated to the document's risk level.

Next: what lawyers look for that AI misses
Step 3 of 6What lawyers catch that AI misses
4 min
Clause review — what qualified review adds
Issue type
Why AI misses it
Jurisdiction validity — a clause that is enforceable under English law but unenforceable under Norwegian law
AI models trained on English-language legal text will often default to English law structures that are not valid in other jurisdictions.
Outdated statutory references — citing a regulation that has been amended or replaced
Training data has a cutoff. Recent legislative changes — including the EU AI Act and the Digital Omnibus — may not be reflected.
Missing mandatory clauses — a contract that omits a clause required by law for that contract type
Completeness of mandatory clauses requires knowledge of the specific legal regime. AI may produce a plausible document that is still incomplete under the applicable law.
Commercial imbalance — a liability clause that sounds balanced but substantially favours one party
AI optimises for linguistic clarity and structure, not for commercial fairness or strategic advantage. Imbalanced terms may read as professionally appropriate.
Interaction effects — two clauses that read correctly individually but contradict each other
AI generates clause by clause — it may not catch interactions between clauses that were generated at different points or taken from different templates.
The professional liability point: In most jurisdictions, providing a legal document that turns out to be incorrect can create liability for the providing party — even if a disclaimer was included. "This was AI-generated" is not a defence to a claim that you provided a defective legal document. The approval boundary for legal drafts protects the organisation, not just the client.

Step 4 of 6Writing the approval boundary into the brief
3 min

The brief must classify the document and name the approval path

Legal draft agent — brief excerpt

DOCUMENT TYPE: NDA (mutual, standard template)
RISK CLASSIFICATION: Medium — external party
APPROVAL GATE: Qualified lawyer review required before execution
JURISDICTION: Norwegian law, Oslo courts
TEMPLATE: Use Nordvik standard NDA template v3 (approved by legal 2026-06-01)
AI ROLE: Draft only. Fill in party names, definitions, and confidentiality period from the provided inputs. Do not modify structural clauses. Do not add clauses outside the template.
PROHIBITED: Do not modify liability cap, dispute resolution, or termination clauses. Do not add clauses not in the template.
OUTPUT STATUS: This draft must be labelled "DRAFT — REQUIRES LEGAL REVIEW BEFORE EXECUTION" on every page.
The three constraints that matter: (1) AI role is "draft only" — never "final." (2) Structural clauses are locked — the agent fills details into a pre-approved template, not free-drafts. (3) Output is labelled as a draft requiring review — so nobody can sign it thinking it's the final document.

Step 5 of 6Practice
3 min

A startup uses an AI agent to generate a standard SaaS subscription agreement for a new enterprise customer. The agent produces a well-structured 12-page document. The CEO reviews it, finds it "looks professional," and sends it to the customer for signature without a lawyer seeing it. The customer's legal team finds that the limitation of liability clause caps liability at the monthly subscription fee — which for an enterprise deal is €800/month. The customer requests a higher cap before signing. The startup's legal counsel, when finally consulted, confirms the clause is commercially indefensible for a deal of this size.

What approval boundary failure occurred, and what was the root cause?
A
The AI generated a defective document — a better model would have used a higher liability cap.
B
The approval boundary was skipped. A medium-risk external contract requires lawyer review before execution. The CEO's review ("looks professional") is the wrong approval gate — a non-lawyer cannot catch the commercial imbalance in a liability clause. The root cause: no approval boundary was defined, so none was applied. The output went directly from AI to customer without the required qualified review.
C
The CEO should have negotiated the liability cap before sending — this is a commercial negotiation error, not a governance failure.
Final test
Step 6 of 6Pass — hidden test
3 min

A legal team implements a policy: "All AI-generated contracts must be reviewed by a qualified lawyer before execution." After 3 months, the team notices that lawyers are spending significant time reviewing routine NDA amendments that are clearly within the standard template — changing a party name, updating an address, extending a confidentiality period. The head of legal asks: "Is this the right use of lawyer time, or can we create a lower-risk category?"

What is the correct governance response?
A
The current policy is correct — all AI-generated legal output requires lawyer review without exception.
B
The head of legal is right to ask. A risk-based approach can create a "routine amendment" category for changes that: (a) are limited to defined fields (names, dates, addresses), (b) use a pre-approved template with locked structural clauses, and (c) are confirmed by the agent to not have modified any structural clause. These can be reviewed by a trained senior paralegal or manager rather than a qualified lawyer — freeing lawyer time for medium and high-risk documents. This is the risk matrix from Step 2 applied to a sub-category.
C
The policy should be removed — if lawyers are spending too much time on routine items, the overhead outweighs the benefit.
