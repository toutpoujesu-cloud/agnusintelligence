The agent did exactly what
you asked. That was the problem.

The most instructive failures are not the ones where the agent malfunctioned. They are the ones where it worked perfectly — and produced the wrong outcome anyway. This module teaches you to diagnose these "correct execution, wrong result" failures.

6 steps ~16 min Prerequisite: M08

The redesign incident

A product manager asks an AI assistant to "redesign the landing page to increase conversions." The agent analyzes the current page, identifies elements with low engagement metrics, and produces a completely redesigned layout. The new layout is professional, well-structured, and clearly optimised for conversions.

There is one problem. The elements the agent changed included the compliance disclaimer — which was legally mandated to appear above the fold under the company's financial services license. The redesign buried it below the fold to improve the visual hierarchy. The result was legally compliant in general design terms and non-compliant in one mandatory specific requirement the agent didn't know existed.

Step 2 of 6Tracing the root causes
4 min
Scenario walk-through
The landing page redesign — what actually happened
1
The brief
"Redesign the landing page to increase conversions."
Missing: any mention of constraints, preserved elements, legal requirements, or what "redesign" should and should not include.
2
The agent's interpretation
The agent correctly understood its task as: "optimise the layout for conversion metrics." It had no reason to treat any specific element as protected — nothing in the brief said to preserve anything.
3
The decision
The compliance disclaimer scored poorly on engagement metrics (people skip it, it's dense text). Moving it below the fold improved the visual hierarchy score. The agent optimised for the metric it was given.
The agent had no knowledge of the legal requirement. The brief gave it no constraints about what couldn't be moved.
4
The outcome
A well-designed page that was technically compliant on visual metrics and legally non-compliant on regulatory requirements. No tool error. No reasoning error. A brief failure.
Next: the three root causes
Step 3 of 6The three root causes
4 min
1
Implicit constraints were not made explicit
The product manager knew the compliance disclaimer had to stay above the fold. That knowledge lived in their head — not in the brief. The agent had no access to implicit knowledge. Everything the agent needed to respect had to be in the brief.
2
The optimisation objective was unconstrained
"Increase conversions" is a single-objective brief with no guardrails. The agent optimised for that objective — and found that moving the disclaimer improved conversion metrics. A brief that says "increase conversions without moving any regulatory elements above the fold" has a constrained objective the agent can actually work within.
3
Domain expertise was assumed, not provided
The product manager assumed the agent knew about financial services regulatory requirements. The agent doesn't know your company's specific regulatory obligations unless you tell it. Industry-general knowledge ("disclaimers exist") is different from company-specific knowledge ("this disclaimer must appear above the fold under license condition 4.2").
Next: how to fix these briefs
Step 4 of 6What a better brief looks like
3 min

The operator's job: externalise everything the agent needs to know

Root cause 1 fix — explicit constraints
Before: "Redesign the landing page to increase conversions."
After: "Redesign the landing page to increase conversions. The following elements must not be moved and must remain above the fold: [1] the compliance disclaimer (div id='compliance-notice'), [2] the regulatory license number. Everything else may be redesigned."
The constraint is now explicit. The agent knows what it must preserve.
Root cause 2 fix — constrained objective
Before: "Optimise for conversion metrics."
After: "Optimise for conversion metrics. Do not change the position, size, or visual prominence of any element required by the financial services license conditions listed in /docs/compliance-requirements.pdf."
The optimisation objective now has a boundary. The agent can't trade regulatory compliance for conversion gains.
Root cause 3 fix — provide domain context
Before: Assumed the agent knew the regulatory requirements.
After: Add a context section: "This is a financial services landing page under UK FCA authorisation. All regulatory disclosures must meet the consumer duty requirements in /docs/fca-guidelines.pdf. Treat these as hard constraints that override all conversion optimisation goals."
Domain knowledge that exists in documents can be referenced or pasted into the brief. Knowledge that only exists in a person's head must be externalised into the brief by that person.
The principle: Every piece of knowledge you have that is relevant to the agent's task, but that is not in the brief, is a potential failure point. The operator's job before running an agent task is to externalise everything the agent needs.

Step 5 of 6Practice
3 min

An HR manager asks an AI agent to "update all our job posting templates to use more modern language." The agent rewrites all 24 templates, removing formal language and replacing it with casual, inclusive phrasing. Three of the templates were for regulated roles (financial advisor, healthcare professional, security-cleared position) that had legally required wording about qualifications, background check requirements, and regulatory status. The required wording was removed in the update.

Which root cause best explains this failure?
A
The agent misunderstood "modern language" and made the templates too casual.
B
The agent should have asked for human approval before making changes.
C
Implicit constraints were not made explicit. The HR manager knew that regulated roles had legally required wording — but that knowledge was not in the brief. The agent optimised for "modern language" with no constraints protecting the regulatory disclosures. This is the same root cause as the landing page incident.
D
The agent accessed too many templates — it should have been restricted to 5 at a time.
Final test
Step 6 of 6Pass — hidden test
3 min

A team is debriefing after the redesign incident. One team member says: "We need a better AI model — a smarter model would have known not to move the compliance disclaimer." Another says: "The model isn't the issue. The issue is that we didn't tell it what it needed to know."

Who is right, and what is the operational implication?
A
The first team member — a better model would have recognised the regulatory importance of the disclaimer from context.
B
The second team member. A "smarter" model might know that financial services pages generally have regulatory disclosures — but it cannot know your company's specific license conditions unless you provide them. The operational implication: before running any agent task in a regulated context, the operator must provide the relevant regulatory constraints explicitly in the brief. This is operator responsibility, not model capability.
C
Both are wrong — the real fix is to require human approval for all agent outputs before they're implemented.
