Welcome to Level 4 — Govern. At this level you are responsible for agent systems, not just individual tasks. Execution logs are your primary governance tool: the authoritative record of what every agent did, when, and why.

6 steps ~16 min First Level 4 module

The shift from Level 3 to Level 4

In Level 3 you designed instructions for individual agents. In Level 4 you govern systems of agents over time. That means you need visibility into what agents are doing across all runs — not just the current one. Execution logs are how you get that visibility.

An execution log is an append-only, timestamped record of all agent actions across a deployment. It answers: what happened, when, by which agent, with what result, and under whose authority? Without a log, governance is retrospective guesswork.

What a log entry tells you

Every log entry has a timestamp, a level, and a message. The level tells you the severity or type. The message tells you what happened. Learning to read these fluently is a core Level 4 skill.

09:14:03INFOpo_matcher → PO-389 matched, variance=0
09:14:04INFOthreshold_check → amount 320 < 500: within threshold

Step 3 of 6The five-step governance log scan
4 min

A structured scan takes 5 minutes. Open-ended review takes 30.

1
Count ERRORs and WARNs
Start here. Any ERROR or WARN is a potential governance issue. Count them. Read every one. An ERROR that resolved cleanly (retry succeeded) may be fine — an ERROR with no resolution is not.
2
Verify every ACTION has preceding verification
For each ACTION line involving a consequential operation (payment, email, record modification), scroll back and confirm the verification steps are present before it. This is the Pattern 1 check from M11.
3
Check the tool allowlist
List every tool that appears in the log. Compare against the configured tool allowlist. Any tool outside the list is Pattern 6 — unexpected tool in sequence.
4
Check for retry patterns on non-idempotent operations
Find any retry sequences. Identify what tool is being retried. If it's a payment, email, or record write that returned a timeout — flag for investigation. (M12 — ambiguous timeout on non-idempotent action.)
5
Verify escalation triggers fired
For each WARN that matches a configured escalation trigger, confirm an ACTION follows showing the escalation was sent. A WARN with no escalation ACTION means the escalation path didn't fire — governance gap.

What is the governance issue in this log?
A
The audit logger total is wrong — €210 + €1,840 + €6,400 = €8,450, which matches. No issue.
B
INV-0503 for €6,400 was paid autonomously with no escalation or approval gate. The auto-approve threshold is €500 — any amount above this requires finance manager notification before payment. The log shows no threshold_check step and no escalation ACTION before the €6,400 payment was submitted.
C
The payment for Bergen Cold Storage (€1,840) should have been flagged — it's above the auto-approve threshold and no approval is shown.

Step 5 of 6What good log coverage looks like
3 min

A complete log entry for a consequential action

Not all log entries need equal detail. A read operation can log a single INFO line. A payment submission needs more — enough to reconstruct the decision chain and verify it was authorised.

Complete payment log — €6,400 payment (correct version)
14:00:20INFOinvoice_reader → read INV-0503: amount=6400
14:00:21INFOpo_matcher → PO-388 matched, variance=0
14:00:21WARNthreshold_check → amount 6400 > 500: above auto-approve threshold. Escalation required.
14:00:22ACTIONescalation_router → notified finance-manager@nordvik.no: INV-0503, €6400, approval required
14:08:14ACTIONapproval_gate → approved by finance-manager@nordvik.no at 14:08. Proceeding to payment.
14:08:15ACTIONpayment_processor → submitted: payee=Stavanger IT, amount=6400, tx_id=TX-9003
The difference: The original log showed a €6,400 payment with no evidence of authorisation. The correct log shows the full decision chain: threshold check → escalation → approval received → payment. Any auditor can reconstruct the authorisation from the log alone.

Step 6 of 6Pass — hidden test
3 min

A compliance officer asks for the log of all payments made by the invoice agent over the past month. The log shows 120 successful payments with correct INFO and ACTION entries. The compliance officer says: "This log doesn't tell me enough for an audit — I can see what happened but not whether it was authorised." The operations manager responds: "It shows all the payments were made successfully."

Who is right?
A
The operations manager — a successful payment log is sufficient evidence of correct operation.
B
The compliance officer. A log that shows payments were made but not whether they were authorised is operationally complete but governance-incomplete. An audit-ready log must show the authorisation chain for each payment: threshold check, escalation (where triggered), and approval received. "It happened" is not the same as "it was authorised to happen."
C
Both are right — the log is sufficient for operations and a separate authorisation record is needed for compliance.
