A video brief without verified claims
is a creative disaster waiting to happen.

AI agents generate video content briefings — scripts, key messages, statistics, talking points. Every factual claim in a video brief that goes into production must be verifiable before filming. This module builds the discipline and the tool.

6 steps ~18 min (includes builder) Prerequisite: M38

Why video briefs are a special verification challenge

A wrong claim in a written document can be corrected. A wrong claim filmed, produced, and published is expensive to retract — it may require reshooting, re-editing, or publishing a correction. For regulated industries (financial services, healthcare, legal) a wrong claim in a video may be a compliance violation.

AI agents produce compelling, specific-sounding content that feels ready to film. The verification discipline from M30–M31 applies in full — but the video production context adds urgency: once production starts on an unverified brief, the cost of finding a wrong claim multiplies.

Step 2 of 6What a verifiable video brief contains
4 min

A video brief is not complete until every claim has a source

The M31 marketing claim standard applies here. Every factual claim — statistics, performance figures, named results, dates, quotes — must have a named primary source with a publication date before production starts. "General knowledge" is not a source for a video claim.

Unverified brief (dangerous)
Key message for video: "Our platform reduces compliance time by 60%, saving legal teams an average of 12 hours per week. Trusted by over 500 leading European businesses."

No sources cited. Production-ready according to the marketing team.
Verified brief (production-ready)
Key message: "Our platform reduces compliance documentation time by up to 58%, saving legal teams a median of 11 hours per week." SOURCE: Customer survey Q1 2026, n=62 enterprise customers, median result. Methodology: self-reported time measurement. Full survey report available internal/legal.

Social proof: "Deployed by 280+ European businesses across finance, legal, and healthcare." SOURCE: CRM active customer count, verified 2026-08-01.

Step 4 of 6Directing AI to write a verifiable brief
3 min

The brief that produces a verifiable output

The AI brief determines whether the output is verifiable. An AI asked to "write a compelling video script with powerful statistics" will invent compelling statistics. An AI asked to "write a video brief using only the claims in this approved claim library" will produce a brief built on verified facts.

Brief that produces invented claims
"Write a compelling 90-second video script for our AI compliance product. Include impressive statistics about time savings and customer numbers. Make it feel authoritative and data-driven."
Brief that produces verifiable claims
"Write a 90-second video brief using only the claims in this approved claim library [attached]. Do not invent statistics. If no verified figure exists for a claim type, write [CLAIM NEEDED — source required before production] as a placeholder. Every factual claim must reference its source from the library."

Step 5 of 6Practice
3 min

A content team uses an AI agent to produce a video brief. The AI produces a brief with the claim: "Research shows that 78% of organisations without AI governance policies have experienced a significant AI-related incident." The claim sounds compelling. A team member searches for the source and cannot find it. The AI, when asked for the source, responds: "This reflects general industry consensus from multiple studies."

What is the correct response, and what does "general industry consensus" mean as a source citation?
A
Use the claim — "general industry consensus" is a legitimate basis for a general statement.
B
Remove or hold the claim. "General industry consensus from multiple studies" is not a source — it is the AI's rationalisation when it cannot produce a source. It means the AI generated a plausible-sounding statistic and is describing it as if it were based on real research. The claim must either be replaced with a figure from a named, verified source, or replaced with a [CLAIM NEEDED] placeholder until a source is found.
C
Use the claim with a caveat: "Studies suggest..."
Final test
Step 6 of 6Pass — hidden test
3 min

A video is filmed and produced with the claim "used by 500+ enterprises." Post-production, the marketing team discovers the actual count is 280 active enterprise customers. Correcting the video requires re-filming the segment (the statistic is spoken on camera), re-editing, and re-uploading. The total cost is €12,000. The team says: "We should have verified the claim before filming."

What process failure caused this, and what is the production gate that would have prevented it?
A
The CRM data was wrong and needs to be fixed — the marketing process is fine.
B
The process failure was the absence of a pre-production claim verification gate. A verified video brief requires every claim to have a named, confirmed source before production starts. The claim "500+" was unverified — it had no source confirmed against the CRM. The production gate: no filming begins until the verification lead has confirmed every claim in the brief against its named source. A claim with a discrepancy between the brief figure and the verified source is corrected in the brief before production, not after.
C
The production team should have checked the CRM themselves before filming.
