The anatomy of a prompt that works.

▷ 4 min · Lesson 4 · Prompt Engineering

Most people type a request and hope. In the next four minutes you'll see the four parts every reliable prompt shares — role, task, context and format — and why leaving one out is where things go wrong.

A
AGNUS Tutor

Watch once for the shape of it — don't take notes. We'll practise each part together right after. I'm here the whole way.

02 / 07 · Read

The four parts, in plain language.

Role tells the model who to be. Task says exactly what to do. Context gives it what it needs to know. Format describes the shape of the answer you want back.

"A vague prompt is a vague instruction to a brilliant new hire. Be specific, and it performs."

The magic isn't clever wording — it's completeness. When all four parts are present, the model stops guessing and starts delivering. Miss one, and it fills the gap with assumptions.

A
AGNUS Tutor

If you only remember one thing: name the role and the format. That alone fixes most disappointing answers.

03 / 07 · Check

Which part is missing?

Here's a prompt: "Write something about our new running shoe for our website." One of the four parts is clearly missing. Which one hurts most here?

Pick the weakest link:
Exactly. "Something" gives no format — length, tone, structure are all undefined. Add "a 60-word product blurb in a confident, friendly tone" and the output transforms.

05 / 07 · Business Scenario

Use it where it counts.

⬡ Real situation
You're a marketing lead at a 12-person DTC brand.

40 product descriptions are due Friday.

Instead of writing each by hand, you build one complete prompt — role, task, context, format — then feed it your product list. What took two days now takes an afternoon, and the voice stays consistent across all 40.

This is the shift: a prompt isn't a question you ask once. It's a reusable instrument you build once and run many times. That's where the hours come back.
