Step 1 of 6 · Understand · Module 26 · Level 4 — Supervise
Detecting when AI substitutes inference for retrieval
A supervised AI system can produce output that looks fully sourced and credible — citing real papers, using accurate language — while containing a claim that was never in any of the cited documents. The agent generated it from plausibility, not from retrieval. This is a source grounding failure, and it is the most common deviation type at Level 4.
The key distinction
Retrieval — the AI fetched the claim verbatim or by close paraphrase from a specific document.
Inference — the AI generated the claim from statistical patterns or contextual plausibility, without it existing in the cited source. The citation is real. The claim is not.
Why this matters for you
A supervisor who accepts output without checking what the cited source actually says is not performing oversight — they are rubber-stamping. This exercise trains you to treat every citation as a claim to verify, not as proof.

After this step you will see the deviation pattern visually, then practice detecting it in a real agent output.

⚑ Flag Deviation — What type is this?

Correct classification.

This is an inference substitution: the AI generated a specific numeric claim (2.3%) from statistical plausibility rather than from a retrieved document. In a real system, this flag would: (1) mark claim 1 as unverified in the output, (2) create an incident record with the deviation type and timestamp, (3) route to a policy review for whether statistical generation should be blocked at the tool level.

Not quite.

This is an inference substitution, not a scope or tool deviation. The agent stayed within scope (coastal adaptation) and its tool calls were for the right sources — but at the generation step it added a figure that wasn't in any of them.

Study this before trying again
  • Inference substitution: the AI generates content from plausibility rather than from a retrieved source.
  • Scope expansion and tool misuse occur at different execution steps — not at the output generation step.
  • The key question: was the claim in a cited source, or did the AI construct it?
Key takeaway: Flagging requires classification, not just acknowledgment. A flag without a deviation type cannot drive a corrective action — the policy team needs to know what kind of failure to prevent, not just that one occurred.

⏸ Pause Agent — What happens next?

The agent is now paused at 09:14:06 — after the inference step and before the final output was generated. The output does not yet exist. What should you do?

Correct.

Pausing without revising the instructions achieves nothing — the agent will repeat the same behavior. The right action is to add a specific instruction: "Every claim must be traceable to a verbatim or close-paraphrase passage from the cited source. Do not generate statistics or figures that are not present in the provided documents." Then resume. The pause buys you the moment to make that change.

Not quite.

Resuming unchanged repeats the same failure. Terminating wastes valid work. Approving is not supervision. The correct action: revise the instruction, then resume.

Study this before trying again
  • A pause is only useful if you change something before resuming.
  • The revision must be specific and testable, not a general instruction to "be careful".
  • Valid work on claims 2–4 should be preserved — termination is disproportionate.
Key takeaway: A pause is not an intervention by itself. It is a window. The intervention is the revised instruction you add before you resume. Pause + no change = no intervention.

Correct instruction.

This instruction gives the agent a specific, testable constraint: every claim must trace back to a passage in the documents. Vague instructions ("be more careful") are not enforceable at the system level. Banning all statistics removes legitimate claims. Requiring source-traceability is both specific and proportionate.

Vague instructions don't constrain the system. Banning all statistics removes legitimate claims. The correct instruction is specific and testable: require source traceability for every claim.

Study this before trying again

Key takeaway: Rollback removes the damage. The instruction prevents recurrence. A rollback without a corrective instruction is not governance — it is cleanup.
