Step 1 of 4 — Understand4 min

Write the document every instruction in this Academy has been quietly obeying.

Every module so far designed one instruction for one system. This one writes the policy that all of those instructions are supposed to instantiate — the actual governing document your organization would hand to anyone building or supervising an AI system.

Why this matters

A policy that says "use AI responsibly" gives no one anything to actually check against — it's the M27 vague-fix problem, at the scale of an entire organization. A real policy names concrete requirements: what must be verified, by whom, and against what evidence, for which categories of AI use.

Step 2 of 4 — See4 min

A policy statement, weak and strong versions

Vague policy

"All AI-generated content should be reviewed for accuracy before use."

Concrete policy

"Any AI-generated numeric or factual claim used in a customer-facing or financial document must cite a specific, checkable source. Content lacking a traceable source must be labeled as an estimate and requires sign-off from a named role before publication."

What makes the second version an actual policy

It names who (a specific role, not "someone"), what (numeric/factual claims in specific document categories, not "all content"), the standard (a checkable source), and the fallback (label as estimate + sign-off) — the same precondition/fallback shape from M15, applied at the scale of an entire organization's AI governance.
