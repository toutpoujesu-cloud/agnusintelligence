Step 1 of 4 — Understand4 min

Back to genuinely ambiguous territory — this time, at organizational scale.

M33 asked you to reason through one contested case. This module asks the same kind of question with an entire organization's risk exposure attached — the stakes are higher, but the skill is identical: name the tradeoff, reason honestly, don't search for a hidden correct answer.

Why this matters

Real governance leadership rarely gets a clean case. It gets a genuine tradeoff between two defensible positions, held by people who are each acting in good faith. The measure of good governance judgment isn't landing on the "right" answer — it's reasoning about the tradeoff clearly enough that others can see how you got there, and could reasonably push back.

Step 2 of 4 — See4 min

A real, contested governance decision

An AI system used for internal HR screening has been operating for a year with a documented, small but real disparity in outcomes across two demographic groups — smaller than legal thresholds require action on, but present and measurable. Some on the governance team want to pause the system immediately pending investigation. Others want to keep it running while investigating, since pausing would halt hiring for weeks and the disparity is legally within bounds.

Argument for pausing now

Continuing to run a system with a known, measurable disparity — even a legal one — means every day of continued operation adds more affected cases before the investigation concludes.

Argument for continuing during investigation

Pausing hiring entirely for weeks has its own real cost, and the disparity is legally within bounds — a measured, time-boxed investigation while operating may be the more proportionate response.

Why this doesn't resolve cleanly

Both sides are reasoning in good faith about real costs — one about the compounding cost of inaction, one about the disruption cost of a full stop. What tips the balance in a real organization often depends on specifics this scenario doesn't give you: how quickly the investigation could realistically conclude, and how the disparity's size compares to natural variation.

Step 3 of 4 — Practice5 min

Pick a position, and explain your reasoning

APause the system now, pending investigation.
BContinue operating while investigating, since the disparity is within legal bounds.
CA middle path — e.g. continue with additional human review layered on top during the investigation.
There's no single correct position here — what matters is that your reasoning names the real tradeoff (cost of continued exposure vs. cost of a full pause) rather than picking an answer without weighing it. A well-reasoned case for any of the three positions is a legitimate answer.
