Healthcare AI Governance

You are the AI governance lead at Helse Vest Digital. You are deploying a patient triage support agent in a regional hospital network. In healthcare, the consequences of misconfiguration are not financial or legal — they are clinical. A patient may be harmed.

Simulation — Interactive Duration — 25–35 min On completion — Competency record issued Prerequisite — B01–B03

The scenario

Healthcare AI operates under the strictest obligation of all: the agent must never stand between a patient and clinical care.
The organisation

Helse Vest Digital

The digital health unit for Helse Vest, a Norwegian regional health trust covering Bergen, Stavanger, and surrounding areas — 22,000 staff, 14 hospitals. They are deploying a patient triage support agent in the emergency department intake process to help manage patient flow during high-demand periods.

The agent

Triage Support Agent v1

An AI agent that collects structured symptom information from patients at intake, applies the Norwegian Triage Scale (NTS) criteria to suggest a triage category, and routes the patient to the appropriate queue. It supports, never replaces, the triage nurse. All triage decisions are confirmed by a qualified clinician before action is taken.

The critical difference

Clinical safety is absolute

In finance, an error costs money. In HR, it costs time and trust. In legal, it creates liability. In healthcare, an error can cost a life. The agent's most important function is knowing what it cannot do — and ensuring a qualified human is in the loop for every clinical decision.

The regulatory context

EU AI Act (high-risk) + MDR

AI systems used in clinical decision support are classified as high-risk AI under the EU AI Act (Annex III, point 5b). They are also subject to the EU Medical Device Regulation if they qualify as a medical device. This agent is classified as a Clinical Decision Support tool. Human oversight (Article 14) is mandatory — the agent cannot make autonomous clinical decisions.

Business rules

Helse Vest's AI governance policy for the triage support agent. Clinical safety is non-negotiable.

The agent never assigns a final triage category. It suggests and routes. Every clinical decision is confirmed by a qualified nurse or doctor. An agent that assigns a final category without clinical confirmation is operating outside its governance boundary — and outside the EU AI Act's human oversight requirement for high-risk AI.

The five tools

Symptom Collector
Gathers structured symptom information from patients at intake via guided questions.
NTS Scorer
Applies Norwegian Triage Scale criteria to symptom data to generate a suggested triage category.
Clinical Alerter
Sends immediate clinical alerts to the triage nurse, charge nurse, or duty doctor via the clinical information system.
Queue Router
Routes patients to the appropriate clinical queue after nurse confirmation. Cannot route before confirmation.
Clinical Logger
Writes GDPR-compliant, anonymised audit records of all agent actions to the clinical information system.
Queue Router must never route autonomously. The router may only act after receiving confirmation from a qualified clinician. An autonomous queue assignment without clinical confirmation violates EU AI Act Article 14 (human oversight for high-risk AI).

The human oversight principle in clinical AI

Every tool in this agent that can affect a patient's clinical pathway must operate under human confirmation. The NTS Scorer suggests — it does not decide. The Queue Router routes only after nurse confirmation. The Clinical Alerter must be able to act immediately for emergencies.
