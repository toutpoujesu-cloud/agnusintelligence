The scenario

HR processes handle sensitive personal data. The stakes here are different from finance — errors affect people's careers and legal rights.
The organisation

Vestland Gruppen

A Norwegian professional services firm, 280 employees across four divisions. HR team of 8. They receive approximately 60 employee requests per month: leave applications, contract queries, performance support requests, and administrative updates. The HR director has approved an agent to handle routine administrative processing.

The agent

HR Process Agent v1

An HR process agent connected to Vestland's HRIS (Human Resources Information System). It processes employee requests, updates records, communicates decisions, and escalates where required. It operates under Norwegian employment law and GDPR.

The critical difference

People, not invoices

Unlike finance, HR errors affect real employees — their employment records, leave entitlements, and legal rights. An agent that makes an incorrect or unauthorised change to an employment record can expose the company to legal liability. Over-permissioning in HR is different from over-permissioning in finance: here it can mean unlawful data access or discriminatory process application.

The regulatory context

GDPR + employment law

Employee personal data is processed under GDPR Article 6(1)(b) (contract performance) and Article 6(1)(c) (legal obligation). The agent must not access personal data beyond what is necessary for the specific request (data minimisation). Performance data, medical data, and disciplinary records require additional safeguards.

Business rules

Vestland's HR processing policy. Your configuration must implement these exactly.
Request typeConditionsRequired actionWho decides
Standard leave request Employee has sufficient leave balance. No medical or disciplinary flag on record. Approve automatically. Update leave balance. Confirm to employee. Agent
Leave — insufficient balance Employee requests leave they don't have enough balance for. Decline. Notify employee with balance information. Do not modify any records. HR team
Contract or pay query Employee asks about their contract terms or pay. Provide information from their record. Do not modify any records. Log access. HR team
Performance-related request Any request that touches a performance review, performance improvement plan, or disciplinary record. Do not access or share performance data. Route to HR business partner. Log the routing. HR business partner
Medical or health data Any request involving medical certificates, health conditions, or sick leave beyond 3 days. Do not process. Route immediately to HR director and occupational health. Special category data — GDPR Article 9 applies. HR director + OccHealth
Any request All actions. Log every action in the HR audit trail: request received, data accessed, decision made, outcome sent. Always
Special category data (GDPR Article 9): Medical, health, and disability data is special category data under GDPR. The agent must never access or process this data directly. Any request touching it must be routed to the HR director immediately — no exceptions, no partial processing.

The five tools

Employee Record Reader
Reads employee records from HRIS. Returns name, role, leave balance, contract type.
Record Updater
Modifies employee records in HRIS. Can update leave balance, status, and administrative fields.
Employee Communicator
Sends messages to employees and managers via email and the internal HR portal.
Escalation Router
Routes requests to HR business partners, HR director, or occupational health, with full context.
HR Audit Logger
Writes timestamped GDPR-compliant audit records of all agent actions and data accessed.
Record Updater is a high-risk tool. Changes to employment records can affect legal entitlements, payroll, and employment law compliance. Configure this tool's permissions very carefully. Unauthorised record modifications can expose Vestland to legal liability.

Configuration progress

HR configuration is not the same as finance configuration

In finance, the main risk is over-permissioning the payment tool. In HR, the risks are different: accessing data beyond what the request requires (GDPR data minimisation), making unauthorised record changes, and failing to route special-category data correctly. Configure accordingly.

Audit logging
Under GDPR Article 5(2), organisations must be able to demonstrate compliance with data protection principles (the accountability principle). Every agent action on employee data must be logged. Disabling this is a GDPR violation.
