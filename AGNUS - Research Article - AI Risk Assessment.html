AI Risk Assessment: A Practical Framework for Deployers

Risk assessment is the foundation of responsible AI deployment. Without it, governance controls are not calibrated to actual risk — they are either over-engineered for low-risk systems or insufficient for high-risk ones. This article maps a practical risk assessment framework that works for both the NIST AI RMF's MAP function and the EU AI Act's deployer obligations.

Jean-Daniel Alexis· 11 September 2026· AI Governance pillar· AR-000600

AI risk categories

AI risk is not a single dimension. A comprehensive assessment must consider multiple categories, each of which contributes to the overall risk profile of a deployment.

Risk categoryWhat it coversKey questions
Performance risk The risk that the model produces incorrect, biased, or inconsistent outputs What is the accuracy baseline? What is the error rate in production conditions? What is the distribution of errors across demographic groups?
Safety risk The risk that the system causes physical, psychological, or financial harm to individuals or groups Who is affected by the system's outputs? What is the worst-case harm scenario? Is the system used in safety-critical contexts?
Security risk The risk that the system is compromised, manipulated, or misused What are the attack vectors? What is the blast radius of a compromise? Is the system a target for adversarial manipulation?
Privacy risk The risk of personal data exposure, processing without lawful basis, or inference of sensitive attributes What personal data does the system process? What is the lawful basis? What inferences can it draw about individuals?
Accountability risk The risk that no one is clearly accountable for decisions or outcomes — especially in multi-agent or third-party deployments Who is accountable for each output? Is accountability clear in multi-agent systems? Who is responsible when third-party components fail?
Regulatory risk The risk of non-compliance with applicable regulations — EU AI Act, GDPR, sector regulation Is the system in scope for the EU AI Act? Is it high-risk under Annex III? What sector regulations apply?
Reputational risk The risk that the system's outputs or the circumstances of its use cause reputational damage What is the public perception risk? Could outputs be misrepresented or taken out of context? Is use of AI in this context publicly acceptable?

Scoring likelihood and impact

Each risk category should be scored on two dimensions: likelihood (how probable is the risk materialising) and impact (how severe is the consequence if it does). Both dimensions use a four-point scale in this framework:

Likelihood: 1 — Rare (unlikely given controls); 2 — Possible (could occur under some conditions); 3 — Likely (will occur without specific mitigation); 4 — Near certain (expected to occur without significant controls)

Impact: 1 — Minor (limited consequence, easily remediated); 2 — Moderate (meaningful consequence, manageable); 3 — Significant (serious consequence, difficult to remediate); 4 — Critical (severe consequence, irreversible or catastrophic)

High + Security review, DPIA if personal data, red-team testing, incident response procedure, AI Governance Board review AI Governance Board approval
Critical + Conformity assessment (if EU AI Act high-risk), external audit, board-level accountability, pre-deployment regulatory notification where required C-suite / Board approval

Documenting the assessment

The risk assessment must be documented in a form that can be reviewed by governance stakeholders and, for high-risk AI under the EU AI Act, by supervisory authorities. The documentation should include: the system description (purpose, capabilities, deployment context), the risk category scores with reasoning, the overall risk level determination, the controls implemented to mitigate each risk, and the residual risk level after controls are applied.

The NIST AI RMF Playbook's MAP function provides a structured template for this documentation. The EU AI Act's technical documentation requirements in Annex IV go further — they require detailed information about training data, testing procedures, intended purpose, and known limitations. For systems in scope, the risk assessment is incorporated into the broader technical documentation package.

Risk assessments are living documents. They should be reviewed when the system's purpose, capabilities, or deployment context changes; when a significant incident occurs; and at least annually for all deployments. A system whose risk level has increased since its original assessment must have its governance controls updated accordingly.
