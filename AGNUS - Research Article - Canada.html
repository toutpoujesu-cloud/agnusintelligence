Canada AI Governance: AIDA, Bill C-27 and the Voluntary Code

Canada has been developing the Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA) — Canada's proposed horizontal AI law — since 2022. Included in the broader digital omnibus Bill C-27 alongside updates to PIPEDA, AIDA would be the first comprehensive federal AI law in North America. Its legislative journey has been slow, and as of September 2026, it had not yet passed into law.

AIDA: Canada's proposed AI law

The Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA) was introduced as part of Bill C-27 in June 2022. It has been significantly revised through Parliamentary committee work in 2023 and 2024. As of September 2026, Bill C-27 had passed committee stage but had not received Royal Assent, making AIDA's enactment and timeline uncertain.

AIDA's approach is risk-based, similar to the EU AI Act. It designates certain AI systems as "high-impact" (a concept analogous to "high-risk" in the EU AI Act) and imposes obligations on "persons responsible for" those systems — covering both developers and deployers. Key AIDA provisions include: impact assessments for high-impact systems; risk mitigation measures; monitoring for harms; transparency to affected individuals; notification of harmful outcomes; and record-keeping requirements.

AIDA's definition of "high-impact AI" is broader than the EU AI Act's Annex III list — it delegates the specific classification to regulation rather than including it in the Act itself. This means the full scope of AIDA's obligations will only be clear when the implementing regulations are published, which will follow enactment.

AIDA includes criminal offences for reckless or fraudulent deployment of AI that causes serious harm or that provides false or misleading information to regulators. This criminal liability provision goes further than the EU AI Act, which provides for administrative fines but not criminal penalties.

The Voluntary Code of Conduct for Advanced Generative AI Systems

In the absence of enacted legislation, the Canadian government launched a Voluntary Code of Conduct for Advanced Generative AI Systems in September 2023. The Code asks organisations developing or deploying advanced generative AI to make voluntary commitments covering: safety and security measures; honesty and transparency; human oversight and control; diversity, equity and inclusion; accountability; robustness and security; and responsibility for outcomes.

The Code is administered by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED). Signatories commit to reporting progress annually. As of September 2026, multiple Canadian AI companies and international companies with Canadian operations had signed the Code.

The Voluntary Code is not a substitute for AIDA. It is an interim measure — explicitly temporary in design — intended to demonstrate that Canadian AI developers are taking governance seriously while legislation proceeds. When AIDA is enacted, the Code's requirements are likely to be superseded by AIDA's binding obligations for high-impact systems.

PIPEDA and AI: the current legal framework

Until AIDA is enacted, the primary legal framework governing AI in Canada is the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA), supplemented by provincial privacy laws in Quebec (Law 25), Alberta, and British Columbia. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner (OPC) has published guidance on AI and automated decision-making under PIPEDA.

PIPEDA's accountability principle requires organisations to be accountable for personal information in their custody, including information used to train AI models. The OPC has taken enforcement action against algorithmic systems that caused harm to individuals without adequate accountability measures — most notably in the Cadillac Fairview case involving AI-powered facial recognition in shopping centres.

Quebec's Law 25 (effective November 2023) is significantly more stringent than PIPEDA and includes specific provisions for automated decision-making. Organisations subject to Law 25 must provide explanations of profiling decisions and allow individuals to request human review. Law 25 is closer to GDPR than PIPEDA in its protections — organisations operating in Quebec face a more demanding AI governance environment than those operating only under PIPEDA.

For operators

Monitor AIDA progress. AIDA's enactment will create binding obligations for high-impact AI systems. Organisations developing or deploying high-impact AI in Canada should prepare governance documentation (impact assessments, risk mitigations, monitoring processes) now, rather than waiting for the law to pass.

Quebec Law 25 applies now. Organisations with Quebec operations must comply with Law 25's automated decision-making provisions. This includes providing explanations and human review mechanisms for consequential AI decisions.

Consider signing the Voluntary Code. The Voluntary Code is a credible signal of governance commitment for Canadian AI developers and for international companies operating in Canada. It is also a useful governance self-assessment framework regardless of whether formal commitments are made.
