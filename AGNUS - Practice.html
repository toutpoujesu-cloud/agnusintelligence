EU AI Act Risk Classifier

Answer 7 questions about your AI system. The classifier applies the EU AI Act's risk classification logic and tells you which tier applies, what that means, and what you need to do by when.

Question 1 of 7

Is your AI system intended to be used in any of the following applications?

Social scoring of natural persons by public authorities
Real-time biometric identification in public spaces (law enforcement)
Subliminal manipulation or exploitation of vulnerabilities
Predictive policing based solely on profiling
None of the above
Source: EU AI Act Article 5 (Prohibited AI practices)
Question 2 of 7

Is your AI system used for any of these high-risk applications (Annex III)?

CV screening, candidate shortlisting, or performance evaluation in employment
Credit scoring or insurance risk assessment
Admissions, grading, or assessment in education
AI used by or on behalf of law enforcement or courts
Management of critical infrastructure (water, energy, transport, finance)
Medical device or safety component in regulated product (Annex I)
None of the above
Source: EU AI Act Article 6 + Annex III
Question 3 of 7

Is your system a general-purpose AI (GPAI) model — a model trained on broad data intended to serve many tasks?

Yes — it is a foundation or GPAI model released for general use
Yes — and it has been trained with more than 10²⁵ FLOPs (potential systemic risk)
No — it is a specific-purpose application
Source: EU AI Act Articles 51–56 (GPAI obligations)
Question 4 of 7

Does your AI system interact directly with natural persons (users)?

Yes — users interact with it via chat, voice, or generated content
Yes — it generates synthetic audio, image, or video content
No — it is an internal/back-office system only
Source: EU AI Act Article 50 (Transparency obligations)
Question 5 of 7

What is your organisation's role?

Provider — we develop and place the AI system on the market or into service
Deployer — we use an AI system placed on the market by a third party
Both — we develop for internal use (treated as provider)
Source: EU AI Act Articles 3, 25 (role definitions and obligations)
Question 6 of 7

Where do you operate or place the system on the market?

In the EU, or to EU users / EU market
Outside the EU — but the AI output is used in the EU
Outside the EU — no EU users or market exposure
Source: EU AI Act Article 2 (territorial scope)
Question 7 of 7

When was or will the system be placed on the market?

After 2 August 2026
Before 2 August 2026 — already on the market
Source: EU AI Act Digital Omnibus — transition periods

Provider vs Deployer Self-Assessment

Your obligations under the EU AI Act depend on your role. This tool identifies whether you are a provider, a deployer, or both — and lists the obligations that apply.

Question 1

Did your organisation develop or substantially modify the AI system?

Yes — we developed it from scratch, fine-tuned a foundation model, or made substantial modifications
We integrated an existing model into our product and customised it
No — we use a system developed and placed on the market by a third party
Question 2

Do you place this AI system on the market — i.e., make it available to external customers or users?

Yes — we sell, license, or provide it to other organisations or end users
No — we use it internally for our own purposes only
