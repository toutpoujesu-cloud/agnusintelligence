NIST AI RMF in Practice: How to Implement the Four Functions

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) organises AI risk management into four core functions: GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE. Unlike compliance frameworks that tell you what to do, the AI RMF tells you how to organise the work. This article maps each function to concrete implementation steps and shows where the AI RMF aligns with EU AI Act requirements.

Jean-Daniel Alexis· 11 September 2026· Standards pillar· AR-000710

Overview: four functions, not a checklist

The NIST AI RMF is structured around four core functions that map the lifecycle of AI risk management. They are not a checklist — they are an organisational model for how risk management work should be structured. Each function has sub-categories (actions) and practitioner guidance in the AI RMF Playbook.

Function 1

GOVERN

Establishes the policies, roles, and processes that enable the other three functions to operate. Before you can map, measure, or manage AI risk, you need an organisational structure that is accountable for doing so.

Function 2

MAP

Identifies and categorises AI risks for specific systems in their deployment context. Where and how can this AI system cause harm? Who is affected? What are the dependencies and failure modes?

Function 3

MEASURE

Assesses and tracks identified risks using appropriate metrics and evaluation methods. How severe is each risk? How probable? How are risks changing over time?

Function 4

MANAGE

Implements risk treatments and monitors their effectiveness. What controls reduce each risk? Are those controls working? What do we do when risks change?

GOVERN: Building the accountability structure

The GOVERN function establishes the organisational conditions for AI risk management. Its key actions include GOVERN 1.1 (documenting risk management policies, processes, and procedures), GOVERN 1.2 (identifying roles and responsibilities), GOVERN 1.7 (establishing third-party risk management processes), and GOVERN 6.1 (ensuring risk management processes are funded and staffed).

In practice, GOVERN translates to three deliverables: a written AI governance policy that applies across the organisation; a documented set of roles with named individuals and clear accountabilities; and a governance committee or board that meets regularly to review AI risk. An organisation that cannot point to all three has not implemented GOVERN.

GOVERN 1.7 specifically addresses AI supply chain and third-party AI components — requiring organisations to understand and manage risks from vendors, pre-trained models, and datasets. This is the GOVERN action most directly relevant to organisations deploying third-party AI systems.

MAP: Identifying risks in context

The MAP function identifies risks for a specific AI system in its specific deployment context. The same AI model may present different risks in a healthcare setting versus a financial services setting versus a retail setting. MAP requires context-specific analysis, not just a generic risk taxonomy.

MAP 2.1 requires categorising the AI system according to the contexts in which it will operate, the stakeholders it will affect, and the decisions it will inform. MAP 5.1 requires identifying and documenting third-party components and their associated risks. MAP 5.2 requires assessing the risks of these components to the overall system.

The most common failure in MAP is generalising risks from one context to another. A content moderation AI has different risk profiles in consumer social media, in enterprise collaboration tools, and in medical image classification. The MAP function requires that each deployment context be assessed independently.

MEASURE: Quantifying and tracking risks

The MEASURE function moves from risk identification to risk quantification. MEASURE 1.1 requires establishing and documenting metrics for each identified risk category. MEASURE 2.1 requires testing AI systems against their performance claims before deployment. MEASURE 2.5 requires assessing fairness and bias on relevant subgroups.

MEASURE is where AI risk management most often breaks down in practice. Organisations identify risks (MAP) but do not systematically measure them. They deploy AI systems with performance claims but do not test those claims against the deployment population. They assert fairness but do not measure it across relevant demographic groups.

The AI RMF Playbook provides specific guidance on measurement approaches for each risk category. For bias and fairness, it references multiple fairness metrics (demographic parity, equalised odds, counterfactual fairness) and notes that no single metric is appropriate in all contexts — the choice of fairness metric is itself a governance decision.

MANAGE: Implementing and monitoring controls

The MANAGE function implements risk treatments and monitors their effectiveness over time. MANAGE 2.2 requires documenting risk responses, including the rationale for accepted risks. MANAGE 3.1 requires monitoring risk controls over time and updating them as conditions change. MANAGE 4.1 requires documenting lessons learned from incidents.

MANAGE connects the AI RMF to post-deployment operations. It is the function that operationalises continuous monitoring, incident response, and governance updates. An organisation that completes GOVERN, MAP, and MEASURE before deployment but does not implement MANAGE has completed a pre-deployment assessment — not an ongoing risk management programme.

How the AI RMF aligns with EU AI Act requirements

AI RMF functionPrimary EU AI Act alignment
GOVERN 1.1–1.2 (policies, roles)EU AI Act Article 26 (deployer obligations) + Article 9 (risk management system)
MAP 2.1 (context categorisation)EU AI Act Articles 6–9 (risk classification, Annex III assessment)
MAP 5.1 (third-party components)EU AI Act Article 11 (technical documentation) + Article 28 (value chain obligations)
MEASURE 2.1 (pre-deployment testing)EU AI Act Article 9(8) (testing before market placement)
MEASURE 2.5 (fairness and bias)EU AI Act Article 10 (training data quality + bias) + Article 15 (robustness)
MANAGE 3.1 (ongoing monitoring)EU AI Act Article 72 (post-market monitoring)
MANAGE 4.1 (incident documentation)EU AI Act Article 73 (serious incident reporting)

The AI RMF does not map one-to-one with the EU AI Act — the frameworks have different structures and different scopes. But organisations implementing both can use the AI RMF as an organisational model for how to structure the work, while using the EU AI Act as the compliance specification for what the work must produce.
