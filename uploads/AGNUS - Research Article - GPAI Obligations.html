General-Purpose AI Model Obligations Under the EU AI Act (Articles 51, 53, and 55)

A separate track from the high-risk-system rules covered elsewhere in this pillar: every general-purpose AI model provider faces documentation and transparency duties from Article 53, models crossing a 1025 FLOP compute threshold face additional systemic-risk obligations under Article 55 — and this entire tier has applied since 2 August 2025, a full year ahead of the Act's general application date.

A bank of server racks in a data centre with status lights, evoking the compute-threshold test that determines whether a general-purpose AI model carries systemic risk under Article 51.

    Requirements

    What organizations must do

    • Treat the 1025 FLOP threshold as a planning input, not a retrospective check — the two-week notification clock starts running once the threshold "becomes known" to be met, not only once it is actually met.
    • Confirm all of the Article 53(2) open-source exemption's conditions are met simultaneously before relying on it, and remember it disappears entirely for systemic-risk models.
    • Treat Article 53 compliance as a live obligation, not a future one — Chapter V has applied since 2 August 2025.
    • Request Article 53(1)(b) Annex XII information directly from an upstream model provider before building a downstream system on top of it.

    Common mistakes

    Assuming GPAI obligations only apply to a small number of well-known frontier-model providers is a common mistake — Article 53's baseline obligations apply to providers of general-purpose AI models as a category, with systemic risk under Article 55 as an additional tier for a narrower subset, not a replacement trigger.

    Treating the open-source exemption as a blanket carve-out for any freely available model is a second mistake — the exemption has multiple cumulative conditions and is unavailable entirely for systemic-risk models, regardless of licensing.

    Assuming the GPAI tier's earlier 2 August 2025 application date means its fine regime is equally mature is a third mistake this article specifically corrects — Article 101 was excepted from that date and instead applies from the Act's general 2 August 2026 date, a full year later.

    Confusing a general-purpose AI model's own Chapter V compliance with the Chapter III high-risk classification of a downstream AI system built on top of it is a fourth mistake — the two are separate regimes, and satisfying one does not satisfy the other.

    Open questions

    • Whether Chapter V was genuinely unamended by the Digital Omnibus, given the consistent absence of disclaimers across Articles 51, 52, 53, and 55.
    • What the Commission's published guidelines on the scope of GPAI obligations substantively say — not reviewed on this pass, only their existence was noted.
    • The exact mechanics of Article 52's Commission-designation and reassessment procedure beyond paragraph 1's notification duty — corroborated via a secondary source only, not independently re-verified word-for-word.
