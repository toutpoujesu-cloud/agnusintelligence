New York’s Proposed FOCUS Act: AI Restrictions for Schools and EdTech Providers

New York Senate Bill S10685 — the Fostering Optimal Classroom Use of Screens Act — was introduced and referred to the Senate Rules Committee on 21 August 2026. The bill proposes to limit school-issued devices, prohibit generative AI, conversational AI, and AI companions on those devices, require educational-technology providers to register with the New York Attorney General, and restrict use of student personally identifiable information — including excluding AI model training from permitted internal business operations. It is not enacted law.

Source: NY Senate Bill S10685, introduced 2026-08-21, referred to Senate Rules Committee (SRC-0076, IR-000123). This article covers the bill as introduced. Track amendments and committee action before acting on its provisions.

    What the bill proposes

    The four key proposals

    Proposal 1
    Device limitations on school-issued hardware
    School-issued devices would be limited in what they can access or run. The bill is directed at reducing non-educational content access — a continuation of the screen-time and social media restriction trend at state level. Exact technical specifications of what is restricted are in the bill text; these will be subject to amendment.
    Proposal 2
    Prohibition of generative AI, conversational AI, and AI companions
    The bill would prohibit generative AI, conversational AI, and AI companions on school-issued devices. This is the most significant AI-specific provision. It would require device vendors and EdTech providers to ensure these categories of AI are not accessible on school hardware. The definitions of “generative AI,” “conversational AI,” and “AI companion” will determine scope in practice.
    Proposal 3
    EdTech provider registration with the Attorney General
    Educational-technology providers would be required to register with the New York Attorney General before supplying products to New York schools. Registration typically involves disclosure of data practices, product capabilities, and terms. The registration requirement creates an entry barrier and an enforcement hook: unregistered providers could face enforcement action or school-contract exclusion.
    Proposal 4
    Student PII restrictions — AI training excluded
    The bill would restrict use of student personally identifiable information, specifically excluding AI model training from the “internal business operations” carve-out. Under existing frameworks, internal business operations often permit data use without separate consent. Removing AI training from that carve-out means providers could not use student PII to train AI models even under an internal-operations rationale — consent or another legal basis would be required.

    Who is affected if enacted

    Context: New York AI legislation landscape

    What to monitor

    • Senate Rules Committee action — whether the bill is referred to a substantive committee, amended, or tabled.
    • Companion legislation in the Assembly — New York bills require passage in both chambers; watch for an Assembly companion bill.
    • Definitions of “generative AI,” “conversational AI,” and “AI companion” in the bill text and any amendments — scope depends entirely on these definitions.
    • AG registration framework — what registration requires, how it is administered, and what non-registration consequences are.
    • Student PII provision detail — whether the AI training exclusion applies only to PII or also to de-identified or aggregated student data.
    • Executive action — whether the Governor signals support or opposition, which affects likelihood of enactment.

    What organizations must do now

    • Track the bill’s status via the NY Senate website (SRC-0076). AGNUS Intelligence will monitor primary-source changes and update this record when the bill advances, is amended, or fails.
    • If you supply EdTech products to New York schools: identify whether your product includes generative AI, conversational AI, or AI companions. If so, assess whether a school-device profile excluding those features is technically and commercially viable.
    • Review your student data pipelines: do any AI training processes rely on student PII under an internal business operations rationale? If so, that use would require a separate legal basis if the bill’s PII provision is enacted.
    • Do not act on this bill as if it were enacted law. It is an introduced bill referred to committee. The provisions described here are proposals, not requirements.
