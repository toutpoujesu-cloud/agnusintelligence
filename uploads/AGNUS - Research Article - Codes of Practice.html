Codes of Practice for GPAI Models Under the EU AI Act

Article 56 tasks the AI Office with facilitating the development of voluntary codes of practice for GPAI model providers. Compliance with an approved code creates a presumption of conformity with the Article 53 transparency and documentation obligations and the Article 55 systemic-risk obligations. Codes were due by 2 August 2025. GPAI providers who do not participate must demonstrate compliance with those obligations by alternative means.

A code of practice is a voluntary compliance tool — but non-participation does not mean non-compliance. Providers who stand outside a code must demonstrate compliance with Articles 53 and 55 by other means.
Contents

    Builds on AR-000270 (GPAI Obligations), which covers Articles 53 and 55 in full. This article covers the Article 56 code of practice mechanism as the primary compliance route for those obligations. See also AR-000410 (Standards) for the analogous Article 40 mechanism for high-risk AI systems. Article 56 carries the Digital Omnibus amendment disclaimer.

    Executive summary

    What codes of practice cover

    The three pillars of the mechanism

    Presumption
    Conformity shortcut
    Compliance with an AI Office-approved code creates a presumption of conformity with the Articles 53 and 55 obligations the code covers. The provider does not need to separately demonstrate compliance with those obligations.
    Alternative means
    Non-participation still requires compliance
    A GPAI provider who does not join a code remains bound by Articles 53 and 55. The provider must demonstrate compliance through its own technical documentation, policies, testing records, and risk assessments — without the code providing the framework.
    Fallback
    Commission implementing acts
    If the AI Office concludes that no adequate code of practice is available by 2 August 2025, or that an existing code no longer adequately covers the obligations, the Commission may adopt implementing acts setting out common rules for Articles 53 and 55.

    Requirements and obligations

    What organizations must do

    • Determine whether your AI system qualifies as a GPAI model under Article 3(63) — only GPAI providers are covered by the codes of practice mechanism under Article 56.
    • Monitor the AI Office for publication of approved codes of practice — once published, assess whether participation creates a more efficient compliance pathway than independent demonstration of Articles 53 and 55.
    • If not participating in a code, build your own compliance documentation for Articles 53 and 55: technical documentation for downstream providers, copyright policy, training data summary, and (if systemic risk threshold is met) adversarial testing records, incident reporting procedures, and cybersecurity measures.
    • If your GPAI model meets the 10²³ FLOP threshold or is designated as posing systemic risk, the Article 55 obligations are more demanding — assess whether a code of practice covering those obligations is available and adequate for your model type.
    • Track whether the Commission has adopted Article 56(9) implementing acts — if adequate codes were not available by 2 August 2025 and the Commission acted, those implementing acts may set binding technical rules for Articles 53 and 55 compliance.

    Common mistakes

    Treating codes of practice as optional compliance tools that can be ignored is a common mistake — non-participating providers remain bound by Articles 53 and 55 and must demonstrate compliance by other means. The code of practice is a shortcut to compliance, not an exemption for non-participants.

    Assuming codes of practice cover all GPAI Act obligations is a second mistake — Article 56 codes cover Articles 53 and 55 specifically. Other GPAI-related provisions (including how GPAI models interact with high-risk system requirements) are not covered by this mechanism.

    Treating participation in code development as equivalent to compliance with the code is a third mistake — a provider who contributed to drafting the code but does not formally comply with its requirements does not benefit from the presumption of conformity.

    Failing to monitor whether Commission implementing acts have been adopted under Article 56(9) is a fourth mistake — if codes were inadequate and the Commission acted, binding technical rules may apply to Articles 53 and 55 compliance independently of any code of practice.

    Open questions

    • Whether adequate codes of practice were available by 2 August 2025 — the AI Office aimed for this date; whether the target was met and what codes were published is not confirmed on this research pass.
    • Whether the Commission adopted Article 56(9) implementing acts — depends on whether the AI Office concluded that adequate codes were not available; status not confirmed on this research pass.
    • Whether the Digital Omnibus changed Article 56’s mechanism, timeline, or fallback provisions — Article 56 carries the Omnibus disclaimer; the provisions described here are pre-amendment.
    • How codes of practice interact with third-country GPAI providers who have no EU presence — Article 56 is primarily directed at GPAI providers within the Act’s scope; the enforcement mechanism for third-country providers is through Article 2 and the authorised representative provisions, not through code participation.

    Frequently asked

    FAQ
    Does compliance with a code of practice satisfy all AI Act obligations for a GPAI provider?
    No. Article 56 codes cover Articles 53 and 55. Other obligations that may apply to GPAI providers — such as the Article 2 scope conditions, how a GPAI model interacts with a downstream high-risk AI system, or any sector-specific requirements — are not addressed by the codes of practice mechanism. Compliance with a code addresses a defined subset of GPAI obligations, not the full regulatory picture.
    FAQ
    Can a provider comply with a code of practice without having participated in its development?
    Yes. Article 56 does not restrict access to the presumption of conformity to providers who participated in code development. Any GPAI provider who complies with the requirements of an approved code benefits from the presumption. Participation in development is open to providers who wish to shape the code’s content; compliance with an approved code is open to all.
    FAQ
    What happens if the AI Office concludes a code is no longer adequate?
    Article 56(5) requires the AI Office to ensure regular evaluation and, if necessary, adaptation of approved codes. If an approved code is updated, providers must comply with the updated version to retain the presumption. If the AI Office concludes a code is no longer adequate and cannot be adapted, the Commission’s Article 56(9) implementing-act fallback may be invoked.
