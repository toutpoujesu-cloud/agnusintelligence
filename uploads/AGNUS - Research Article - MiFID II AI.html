AI in EU Retail Investment Services: MiFID II Duties Still Apply

ESMA’s May 2024 public statement makes the position clear: investment firms that use AI when serving retail clients remain subject to all applicable MiFID II requirements — organisational obligations, conduct-of-business duties, and the obligation to act in the client’s best interests. AI does not create a compliance shortcut. The statement is guidance on the application of existing obligations, not a new standalone AI rule.

Source: ESMA Public Statement on AI and investment services, 30 May 2024 (ESMA35-335435667-5924). The statement does not create new law. It clarifies how existing MiFID II duties apply to AI-assisted processes.

    Executive summary

    The three MiFID II pillars

    Pillar 1
    Organisational requirements
    Internal governance structures, systems and controls, management accountability, and the ability to demonstrate that AI-supported processes are supervised, documented, and auditable. AI systems must be explainable to the extent required for governance and regulatory oversight purposes.
    Pillar 2
    Conduct-of-business duties
    Fair treatment of clients, conflicts of interest management, product governance, and suitability/appropriateness assessments. AI-generated recommendations or outputs must satisfy the same conduct standards as human-generated equivalents.
    Pillar 3
    Best-interests obligation
    The MiFID II Article 24 duty to act in the client’s best interests applies to AI-assisted processes. A firm cannot use an AI system that systematically produces outcomes not in clients’ best interests and then attribute the outcome to the algorithm.

    Six AI-specific risks ESMA identifies

    Algorithmic bias
    Systematic skew in outputs due to training data or model design
    Data quality
    Decisions based on incomplete, stale, or unrepresentative data
    Opaque decisions
    Outputs that cannot be explained to clients or supervisors
    Overreliance
    Staff accepting AI outputs without adequate critical review
    Privacy concerns
    Data processing for AI that conflicts with GDPR obligations
    Security concerns
    AI systems as attack surface or as a vector for data breach

    What organizations must do

    • Inventory every AI-supported step in your investment service workflows: where is AI used in customer support, suitability assessment, investment advice, portfolio management, fraud detection, risk management, and compliance?
    • Map each AI-supported step to the applicable MiFID II obligations: which organisational requirements, conduct rules, and best-interests duties apply to this step when performed by a human? Confirm they apply identically when AI performs or supports it.
    • Test for the six ESMA-identified risks in each AI system: bias (test outputs for systematic skew across client groups), data quality (validate inputs), explainability (can you explain a recommendation to a client and to a supervisor?), overreliance (do staff critically review AI outputs?), privacy (GDPR legal basis for data processing), security (vulnerability assessment).
    • Produce your own compliance evidence — do not rely on a vendor’s product assurance or certification as your compliance record. Your firm’s compliance depends on your use, your controls, and your client outcomes.
    • Retain documentation connecting each AI-supported step to MiFID II compliance: governance sign-off, testing results, escalation thresholds, monitoring records. This is the evidence base for regulatory inspection.
    • Monitor for further ESMA guidance: the statement confirmed ongoing monitoring of AI use in investment services. Further guidance on specific AI use cases or risks is likely.
    • Apply the EU AI Act framework in parallel from August 2026: assess whether your investment service AI systems qualify as high-risk under Annex III, and map compliance obligations across both frameworks.

    Common mistakes

    Treating a vendor’s MiFID II compliance assurance as the firm’s own compliance evidence is the most common mistake. Each firm must demonstrate compliance based on its specific use of the system, its client population, and its internal controls — not on generic product claims.

    Limiting controls to client-facing AI is a second mistake. AI that supports internal decisions (portfolio screening, risk scoring, compliance flagging) is within scope even though the client never interacts with it directly. The best-interests obligation follows the decision, not the interface.

    Ignoring the EU AI Act in parallel is a third mistake. From August 2026, both frameworks apply to firms using AI with retail clients. ESMA guidance on MiFID II compliance does not exhaust the regulatory picture.

    Treating overreliance as an acceptable operational norm is a fourth mistake. If staff routinely accept AI outputs without critical review because the volume makes individual review impractical, the firm has a supervision gap that will be visible in any regulatory inspection.

    Open questions

    • Whether ESMA will issue more granular guidance on specific AI use cases (AI-generated suitability assessments, AI-driven portfolio rebalancing, AI customer support scripts) — the May 2024 statement confirmed ongoing monitoring; follow-on guidance expected.
    • How national competent authorities are applying the ESMA statement in supervision — implementation across EU member states varies; firms should check their home NCA’s position and any follow-on supervisory communications.
    • Whether MiFID II will be amended to address AI-specific requirements explicitly — the MiFID II review process is ongoing; AI-specific provisions may be added in a future revision.

    Frequently asked

    FAQ
    Does the ESMA statement create new regulatory obligations?
    No. The statement is guidance on how existing MiFID II obligations apply to AI-assisted investment service processes. It does not add new requirements. However, it signals how ESMA and national competent authorities interpret and will supervise AI use in investment services — firms should treat it as authoritative interpretive guidance that shapes the supervisory expectation.
    FAQ
    Does the MiFID II best-interests obligation apply to AI-generated investment recommendations?
    Yes. ESMA’s position is unambiguous: the obligation to act in the client’s best interests applies to the advice or recommendation delivered, regardless of whether it was generated by a human or an AI system. A firm cannot use a technically compliant AI system as a shield against a best-interests claim if the recommendation was not in the client’s best interests.
    FAQ
    We use a third-party AI tool in our investment service. Are we responsible for its MiFID II compliance?
    Yes, for your firm’s use of the tool and the outputs it produces in your service delivery. Vendor compliance claims cover the product’s design; your firm’s MiFID II compliance depends on how you deploy the product, what client outcomes it produces in your specific context, and what controls you have in place. Due diligence on the vendor is part of organisational requirements; it does not transfer responsibility to the vendor.
