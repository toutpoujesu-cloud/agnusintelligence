Penalties Under the EU AI Act

Article 99 sets maximum fines in three tiers. The Article 5 prohibited-practices tier — covered in AR-000220 — reaches €35 000 000 or 7% of worldwide annual turnover. Operator-obligation violations reach €15 000 000 or 3%. Providing false information to authorities reaches €7 500 000 or 1%. These are ceilings, not fixed fines — the actual amount depends on ten proportionality factors.

Article 99 sets ceilings, not fixed fines. Member States set the actual penalty rules; the fine in any individual case reflects ten proportionality factors including intent, cooperation, and harm.
Contents

    Builds on AR-000220 (Prohibited Practices, Article 5), which covers the highest-tier violations in full. This article covers the Article 99 penalty framework for tiers 2 and 3, and the proportionality factors that govern individual fine decisions across all three tiers. Article 99 carries the Digital Omnibus amendment disclaimer.

    Executive summary

    The three fine tiers

    Tier 1 — Article 99(3)
    €35 000 000
    or 7% worldwide annual turnover — whichever higher
    Violation of Article 5 prohibited AI practices. Covered in full in AR-000220.
    Tier 2 — Article 99(4)
    €15 000 000
    or 3% worldwide annual turnover — whichever higher
    Non-compliance with operator or notified body obligations: Articles 16 (provider), 22 (authorised representative), 23 (importer), 24 (distributor), 26 (deployer), 31/33/34 (notified bodies), 50 (transparency).
    Tier 3 — Article 99(5)
    €7 500 000
    or 1% worldwide annual turnover — whichever higher
    Supply of incorrect, incomplete or misleading information to notified bodies or national competent authorities in reply to a request.

    Requirements

    What organizations must do

    • Map your Article 16/22/23/24/26/50 obligation inventory against the Tier 2 ceiling — each gap represents up to €15M or 3% exposure.
    • Build self-reporting and cooperation processes: Article 99(7)(f) and (h) both weigh in favour of operators who engage proactively with competent authorities.
    • Document harm-mitigation actions following any identified non-compliance — Article 99(7)(j) weighs action to mitigate harm suffered by affected persons.
    • Apply the SME rule correctly: if your organisation qualifies as an SME or start-up, Article 99(6) caps fines at the lower of the percentage and the flat amount — reversing the general whichever-is-higher structure.
    • Track the national penalty rules in each Member State where you operate — the Article 99 ceilings are floors of the ceiling, not uniform fine structures, and national law may introduce additional measures or lower maximums.

    Common mistakes

    Treating the Article 99 ceilings as fixed fines rather than maximums is a common mistake — the actual fine depends on ten proportionality factors; most enforcement outcomes will be significantly below the ceiling.

    Assuming the whichever-is-higher rule applies to SMEs is a second mistake — Article 99(6) reverses this for SMEs and start-ups by applying whichever is lower between the flat amount and the percentage.

    Treating Article 99 as covering only prohibited-practices violations is a third mistake — Tier 2 covers the full Article 16 provider checklist, Article 26 deployer obligations, all supply-chain actor obligations, and Article 50 transparency obligations.

    Assuming national enforcement is uniform across Member States is a fourth mistake — Member States set their own penalty structures within the Article 99 ceilings, and enforcement intensity, non-monetary measures, and procedural frameworks differ by jurisdiction.

    Open questions

    • Whether the Digital Omnibus changed Article 99’s fine tiers, percentages, or proportionality factors — Article 99 carries the Omnibus disclaimer; the amounts described here are pre-amendment.
    • Whether Member States have notified the Commission of their Article 99 penalty rules and what those rules contain — not surveyed on this research pass; national rules differ materially.
    • Whether the Chapter III Section 2 substantive requirements (Articles 9–15) carry independent Tier 2 exposure or only through Article 16(a) — Article 99(4) lists Article 16 explicitly; whether a direct violation of, say, Article 10 (data governance) independent of Article 16 carries Tier 2 exposure is not elaborated in the statute.

    Frequently asked

    FAQ
    For a large company with €10 billion annual turnover, what is the Tier 2 ceiling?
    €300 million (3% of €10B), since that is higher than the €15M flat amount. The whichever-is-higher rule applies to undertakings; the flat amount only operates as the ceiling for operators where the percentage would produce a lower figure.
    FAQ
    Does Article 99(4) cover violations of the Chapter III Section 2 requirements (Articles 9–15) independently?
    Article 99(4) lists Article 16 explicitly. Since Article 16(a) requires providers to “ensure compliance” with Chapter III Section 2, a failure to meet Articles 9–15 is a failure under Article 16(a) and falls in Tier 2. Whether a direct violation of Articles 9–15 independent of Article 16 also falls in Tier 2 is not explicit in Article 99 and was not resolved on this research pass.
    FAQ
    Has the Digital Omnibus changed Article 99’s fine levels?
    Confirmed amended, but the Service Desk has not yet been updated. The fine tiers described here are the pre-amendment version of 13 June 2024.
