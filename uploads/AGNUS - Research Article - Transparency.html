Transparency Obligations Under the EU AI Act (Articles 13 and 50)

Two different transparency regimes, easy to conflate: Article 13's design obligation for high-risk systems to be interpretable by deployers, and Article 50's standalone disclosure duties for chatbots, deepfakes, and AI-generated content — which apply regardless of high-risk status and took effect on 2 August 2026.

A simple "AI-assisted" disclosure label on a printed page, evoking the plain-language disclosure Article 50 requires people to receive when interacting with or exposed to AI systems.

    What organizations must do

    • Treat Article 13 and Article 50 as two separate compliance exercises with different triggers, not a single "transparency" workstream.
    • For Article 50, map each in-scope AI system against the four paragraph-level trigger conditions individually — a system can trigger more than one.
    • Check Article 50 exposure even with no high-risk AI in the portfolio — AR-000210's classification exercise is not a prerequisite.
    • Consult the Commission's published Article 50 implementation guidelines directly for practical labelling and disclosure detail.

    Common mistakes

    Assuming Article 50 only applies to "AI companies" or high-risk-system operators is a common and consequential mistake — any organisation running a customer-facing chatbot, publishing AI-generated marketing content, or using emotion-recognition tooling can be in scope.

    Treating the Article 50(4) evidently-artistic-work exemption as a blanket carve-out for creative content is a second mistake — it only reduces the disclosure, it does not remove the duty entirely.

    Assuming the Article 50(2) assistive-editing exemption covers any AI-assisted content creation is a third mistake — it is narrowly limited to functions that do not substantially alter input data or its semantics.

    Conflating Article 13's deployer-facing instructions-for-use with Article 50's end-user-facing disclosures is a fourth mistake — Article 13 informs the professional deployer; Article 50 informs the natural person who encounters the system.

    Open questions

    • Whether the Digital Omnibus extended the Article 50(2) marking deadline to 2 December 2026 for pre-existing systems. A secondary source claims this, but it was not confirmed against primary text on this research pass.
    • Whether Article 13 was genuinely unamended by the Omnibus, given the absence of a disclaimer on its Service Desk page — not independently verified.
    • What the Commission's 20 July 2026 Article 50 implementation guidelines substantively recommend, beyond the fact of their publication — the guidelines document itself was not fetched on this pass.
