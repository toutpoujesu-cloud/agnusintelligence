South Korea’s New AI Training Data Law: What the PIPC Amendment Means

South Korea’s National Assembly passed a Personal Information Protection Act amendment on 20 August 2026 creating a dedicated statutory route to use lawfully collected personal data for AI development. The amendment is the first such permanent pathway in South Korea, replacing reliance on time-limited regulatory sandboxes. PIPC approval is required before use. Effective date: six months after promulgation. Promulgation is still pending Cabinet consideration.

Source: Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC) press release, 20 August 2026. Discovered via AGNUS Intelligence monitor, 22 August 2026 (IR-000122, SRC-0073). Analysis current as of date of publication; effective date and subordinate legislation pending.

    What the amendment does

    The four conditions

    C1

    Necessity — pseudonymous or anonymous data is insufficient

    The developer must establish that the AI development goal cannot be achieved using pseudonymous or anonymous data. This is a substantive necessity test, not a convenience test. Developers who can achieve their training objective with pseudonymous data must use that route first.

    C2

    Public or social necessity recognised

    The development must serve a recognised public or social necessity. The amendment does not define the threshold for what counts as public or social necessity. This will be clarified in PIPC guidance and through the approval process. Commercial AI development will need to demonstrate how it serves a public or social purpose, not merely private commercial interest.

    C3

    Strengthened safeguards in place

    The developer must implement strengthened safeguards for the personal data used under the special route. What “strengthened” means in practice will be defined in subordinate legislation and PIPC guidance. Expect requirements covering access controls, data minimisation, retention limits, and incident response.

    C4

    PIPC review and resolution obtained

    The developer must submit to PIPC review and obtain a positive resolution before using personal data under the special route. This is a gating step — use cannot begin until PIPC has reviewed and approved the application. The review process, application requirements, and timelines will be defined in implementing rules.

    What organizations must do

    • Monitor PIPC publications for: (1) Cabinet promulgation announcement, which sets the six-month commencement clock; (2) subordinate legislation defining application requirements, risk assessment methodology, and safeguard specifications; (3) PIPC guidance on the public/social necessity threshold.
    • Do not use the new special route before promulgation, subordinate legislation publication, and PIPC review completion. The route is not yet in force.
    • If currently using the AI regulatory sandbox for Korean personal data: confirm with PIPC whether your sandbox approval remains valid pending transition rules or whether it will need to be converted.
    • Begin internal necessity assessment: document why pseudonymous or anonymous data is insufficient for your AI development purpose. This documentation will be the foundation of the PIPC review application.
    • Map the personal data you intend to use under the special route: what was collected, under what original legal basis, from whom, for what purpose. The special route requires lawfully collected data — data collected without a valid legal basis cannot be laundered through the new route.
    • Prepare for public disclosure: the amendment requires transparency through privacy notices and PIPC publication. Plan for public disclosure of AI training data use under the special route before you apply.

    Open questions

    • When promulgation will occur and what the exact commencement date will be — Cabinet consideration is pending as of 23 August 2026.
    • What “public or social necessity” means in practice for commercial AI development — to be defined in PIPC guidance; will determine which developers can access the route.
    • What the “strengthened safeguards” requirement will require technically — to be defined in subordinate legislation.
    • Whether existing sandbox approvals will continue to their expiry or must be converted — PIPC transition rules not yet published.
    • How the PIPC approval process will work in practice — timelines, application form, review criteria — implementing rules pending promulgation.
    • Whether the amendment affects South Korea’s adequacy status with the EU — no EDPB statement as of 23 August 2026.

    Frequently asked

    FAQ
    Can I start using personal data for AI training under the new route immediately?
    No. The amendment has passed the National Assembly but has not been promulgated. The six-month commencement period runs from promulgation, not from National Assembly passage. You cannot use the special route until: (1) the amendment is promulgated; (2) six months have elapsed; (3) implementing rules defining application requirements are published; and (4) you have submitted to PIPC review and obtained a positive resolution. None of these steps have been completed as of 23 August 2026.
    FAQ
    Does the amendment apply to foreign companies processing Korean personal data?
    South Korea’s PIPA applies based on the location of the data subjects, not the location of the processing organisation. Foreign companies that process personal data of Korean individuals are generally subject to PIPA. The special route would therefore be available to qualifying foreign developers, subject to the same conditions and PIPC approval requirement that apply to Korean organisations. The practical mechanics of PIPC review for foreign applicants will be clarified in the implementing rules.
    FAQ
    Does this create a free pass to use any personal data for AI training?
    No. The special route requires lawfully collected personal data — data collected without a valid legal basis cannot be used under this route. The necessity condition, public/social necessity requirement, strengthened safeguards, and PIPC approval create a gated process, not an open permission. Approval is not automatic; PIPC reviews applications before granting access to the route. Transparency through privacy notices and PIPC publication means approvals are not private arrangements.
