# Executive summary

FACT: ESMA states that investment firms using AI when serving retail clients remain subject to relevant MiFID II requirements, especially organisational requirements, conduct-of-business duties and the obligation to act in the client's best interests. SOURCE: SRC-0047, locator: ESMA page paragraphs under the 30 May 2024 announcement.

ANALYSIS: AI does not create a compliance shortcut. A firm should be able to connect each AI-supported step to the same controls, records, accountability and client-protection duties that apply when staff perform the work directly.

## Key takeaways

- FACT: ESMA identifies algorithmic bias, data-quality problems, opaque decision-making, overreliance, privacy concerns and security concerns as relevant risks. SOURCE: SRC-0047, locator: risk bullet list.
- FACT: ESMA identifies customer support, fraud detection, risk management, compliance, investment advice and portfolio management as example AI uses within scope. SOURCE: SRC-0047, locator: paragraph beginning “Potential uses”.
- ANALYSIS: Governance should cover both client-facing outputs and internal AI-supported decisions.

## What this means

FACT: ESMA's statement is guidance on the application of existing MiFID II requirements; it is not presented as a new standalone AI rule. SOURCE: SRC-0046, locator: document metadata; SRC-0047, locator: introductory paragraphs.

ANALYSIS: Firms should test whether AI changes the evidence available to demonstrate suitability, fair treatment, supervision and accountability.

## Who this applies to

FACT: The statement addresses firms using AI technologies when providing investment services to retail clients. SOURCE: SRC-0047, locator: opening paragraph.

## Regulatory context

FACT: ESMA and national competent authorities said they would continue monitoring AI use in investment services and the relevant EU legal framework. SOURCE: SRC-0047, locator: “Next steps”.

## Requirements

FACT: ESMA highlights organisational aspects, conduct of business and acting in the client's best interests. SOURCE: SRC-0047, locator: paragraph beginning “When using AI”.

ANALYSIS: A practical control set should address data quality, bias testing, explainability appropriate to the decision, human escalation, security and monitoring for overreliance.

## What organizations must do

ANALYSIS: Inventory AI-supported investment-service processes; assign accountable owners; document where AI affects client outcomes; validate data and outputs; define human-review and escalation thresholds; and retain evidence showing how MiFID II duties are met.

## Common mistakes

ANALYSIS: Common errors include treating a vendor's assurance as the firm's own compliance evidence, limiting controls to client-facing chatbots, and failing to monitor how staff rely on opaque recommendations.

## FAQ

### Does using AI displace MiFID II duties?

FACT: No. ESMA expects firms using AI to comply with relevant MiFID II requirements. SOURCE: SRC-0047, locator: paragraph beginning “When using AI”.

### Which AI uses does ESMA mention?

FACT: Customer support, fraud detection, risk management, compliance, investment advice and portfolio management. SOURCE: SRC-0047, locator: paragraph beginning “Potential uses”.

### Does the statement prove a particular system is compliant?

ANALYSIS: No. Compliance depends on the firm's use, controls, evidence and client outcomes.
