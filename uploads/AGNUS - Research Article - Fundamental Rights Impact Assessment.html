Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment Under the EU AI Act (Article 27)

A narrower, deployer-side duty than most of Chapter III Section 3: only public-law bodies, private providers of public services, and deployers assessing creditworthiness or pricing life and health insurance must run a fundamental rights impact assessment before first use — and it complements, rather than replaces, an existing GDPR data protection impact assessment.

A public-sector reception desk with a queue of people, evoking the bodies-governed-by-public-law category that anchors much of Article 27's deployer scope.

    Requirements

    What organizations must do

    • Determine whether you fall into one of Article 27(1)'s three deployer categories before assuming a FRIA is required — this test is narrower than general high-risk classification.
    • Map any existing GDPR or Law Enforcement Directive DPIA against the six Article 27(1) elements before starting a FRIA from scratch — Article 27(4) allows the FRIA to complement, not duplicate, that work.
    • Confirm complete Article 13 documentation has been received from the provider before attempting the FRIA — points (d) and (e) depend directly on it.
    • Build a periodic review trigger for the paragraph 2 update obligation rather than treating the FRIA as a one-time, first-use-only exercise.

    Common mistakes

    Assuming the FRIA applies to every deployer of every high-risk AI system is a common mistake — Article 27(1)'s applicability test is deliberately narrow, with critical infrastructure (Annex III point 2) expressly excluded regardless of deployer type.

    Treating the Annex III point 5(c) trigger as covering insurance generally, rather than life and health insurance specifically, is a second mistake — property, casualty, and motor insurance are not named in point 5(c).

    Building the FRIA and an existing DPIA as two entirely separate documents, rather than using Article 27(4)'s complementary relationship, risks duplicated effort and inconsistent risk descriptions.

    Assuming the Article 27(3) notification exemption is broadly available is a fourth mistake — it applies specifically "in the case referred to in Article 46(1)," not as a general opt-out.

    Open questions

    • Whether the Digital Omnibus changed which deployer categories or Annex III points trigger the Article 27 obligation. Article 27's page carries the amendment disclaimer; the deployer-category test cited here is pre-Omnibus text.
    • Whether the AI Office has published the Article 27(5) questionnaire template and automated tool — not confirmed against a primary source on this pass.
    • How a deployer should structure a combined FRIA/DPIA document in practice to satisfy Article 27(4)'s "complement" standard — not addressed by the Regulation's text or any guidance consulted.
