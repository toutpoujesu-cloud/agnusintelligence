ISO/IEC 42001: The AI Management System Standard

ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is the first international standard for AI management systems. It follows the Plan-Do-Check-Act structure used in ISO 27001 (information security) and ISO 9001 (quality), making it familiar to organisations with existing management system certifications. Certification is available from accredited bodies and provides a documented, auditable AI governance record. Not legally required by the EU AI Act — but an increasingly common procurement requirement and a practical implementation framework for Articles 9–15 compliance work.

ISO/IEC 42001 does not certify that your AI system is safe or compliant with the EU AI Act. It certifies that your organisation has a documented, systematic process for managing AI responsibly — and that the process is being followed.

    What ISO/IEC 42001 is

    The Plan-Do-Check-Act structure

    P
    Plan
    Establish the AI management system: define the scope, identify interested parties and their requirements, assess AI-related risks and opportunities, set objectives, and plan the AI impact assessment process. Clauses 4, 5, 6.
    D
    Do
    Implement the AI management system: build the AI policy, allocate resources, establish competence and awareness, implement controls, conduct AI impact assessments, and manage AI system lifecycle activities. Clauses 7, 8.
    C
    Check
    Monitor and evaluate: measure system performance against objectives, conduct internal audits, and review management system effectiveness. Clause 9.
    A
    Act
    Continual improvement: address nonconformities and corrective actions, improve the AI management system based on review findings. Clause 10.

    Key clauses and their EU AI Act mapping

    ISO/IEC 42001 Clause What it requires EU AI Act mapping
    4 — Context Define the scope of the AI management system; identify internal and external factors that affect AI use; identify interested parties (employees, customers, regulators, affected persons) and their requirements. Article 9 (risk management scope); Article 27 (affected persons for FRIA)
    5 — Leadership Top management must demonstrate commitment to the AI management system; establish an AI policy; assign roles, responsibilities, and authorities for AI management. Article 16(a) (provider compliance responsibility); Article 26(1) (deployer governance)
    6.1.2 — AI risk assessment Establish a process for identifying, analysing, and evaluating AI-related risks, including risks to affected persons. Document the assessment and the criteria for evaluating risk significance. Article 9 (risk management system for high-risk AI)
    6.1.4 — AI impact assessment Conduct AI impact assessments to identify potential impacts on individuals, groups, and society; document findings; integrate findings into the AI system design and deployment decisions. Article 27 (FRIA for deployers); Article 9(9) (risk management documentation)
    8 — Operation Implement and control the processes needed to meet requirements; manage AI system lifecycle activities (design, development, deployment, monitoring, decommissioning); control outsourced AI processes. Articles 9–15 (technical requirements); Article 16 (provider checklist); Article 26 (deployer obligations)
    Annex A — Controls Reference control set covering: AI policy; internal organisation; AI system lifecycle; data management; third-party relationships; responsible use; security; human oversight; transparency; explainability. Articles 10–14 (data governance, technical documentation, transparency, human oversight)
    Annex B — Implementation guidance Non-normative guidance on implementing the Annex A controls. Provides examples and explanations for each control category. Informative only; supports Articles 9–15 implementation

    What organizations must do

    • Decide whether ISO/IEC 42001 certification is a strategic objective for your organisation: driven by procurement requirements, regulatory expectation, customer due diligence requests, or internal governance maturity goals.
    • Conduct a gap assessment against ISO/IEC 42001 Clauses 4–10 and Annex A controls. Identify which requirements are already met by existing policies, processes, and documentation, and which require new work.
    • Define the certification scope: all AI systems and activities, or specific AI systems or business units. A narrower scope is faster to certify; broaden incrementally as the management system matures.
    • If you have ISO 27001 certification, explore joint audit scope with your certification body — the Annex SL structure of both standards makes joint audits efficient and cost-effective.
    • Select an accredited certification body: verify accreditation via your national accreditation body (UKAS, DAkkS, COFRAC, etc.). Not all ISO certification bodies are accredited for ISO/IEC 42001 \u2014 confirm before engaging.
    • Build the AI impact assessment process (Clause 6.1.4) as a priority: it maps to both the GDPR DPIA and the EU AI Act FRIA, allowing a single structured assessment to feed both compliance requirements.
    • Document the AI system lifecycle governance for each AI system in scope: design, development, deployment, monitoring, and decommissioning decisions with dated records. This is the core audit evidence for Clause 8.

    Common mistakes

    Treating ISO/IEC 42001 certification as EU AI Act compliance is the most common mistake. Certification confirms your management system is documented and operational. It does not certify that specific AI systems meet Articles 9–15 technical requirements or that your conformity assessment under Article 43 is complete. Both are separate.

    Scoping the certification too broadly at the start is a second mistake. Certifying your entire AI landscape before the management system is mature leads to surveillance audit findings and remediation cycles. Start with a defined, manageable scope and expand as the system strengthens.

    Building ISO/IEC 42001 in isolation from ISO 27001 (if you have it) is a third mistake. The shared Annex SL structure means integration is straightforward. Maintaining two separate management systems for overlapping governance domains creates duplication and inconsistency.

    Selecting an unaccredited certification body is a fourth mistake. ISO certification from a non-accredited body has limited credibility in procurement and regulatory contexts. Verify accreditation status before engaging.

    Open questions

    • Whether CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 will develop an EN version of ISO/IEC 42001 for OJ referencing — alignment work is ongoing between JTC 21 and ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42. An OJ-referenced EN aligned with ISO/IEC 42001 would give certification direct presumption-of-conformity effect under the EU AI Act.
    • Whether the EU AI Act will be amended to reference ISO/IEC 42001 or aligned EN standards explicitly — not confirmed. The Digital Omnibus did not address this.
    • How procurement requirements for ISO/IEC 42001 certification will develop across EU member state public sector buyers — evolving rapidly; no consolidated survey available as of August 2026.
    • How the AI impact assessment in ISO/IEC 42001 Clause 6.1.4 interacts with the GDPR DPIA and the EU AI Act Article 27 FRIA in integrated governance programmes — the three instruments have overlapping purposes but different legal bases. The EDPB guidelines on the GDPR/AI Act interplay (June 2025) do not address ISO/IEC 42001 integration directly.

    Frequently asked

    FAQ
    Does ISO/IEC 42001 certification satisfy the EU AI Act conformity assessment requirement?
    No. The EU AI Act conformity assessment under Article 43 is a legal requirement for high-risk AI systems. ISO/IEC 42001 is a management system standard. Certification confirms that your governance processes are documented and operational. Conformity assessment confirms that specific AI systems meet the technical requirements of Articles 9–15. Both are required for high-risk AI systems. ISO/IEC 42001 certification is useful evidence of governance maturity during a conformity assessment, but it does not substitute for it.
    FAQ
    How long does ISO/IEC 42001 certification take?
    Typically 6–18 months from gap assessment to certification, depending on the organisation’s size, the complexity of its AI activities, and how much of the required management system already exists. Organisations with mature ISO 27001 programmes can often achieve certification faster, as the foundational management system processes are already in place and documented.
    FAQ
    Can a small organisation achieve ISO/IEC 42001 certification?
    Yes. ISO/IEC 42001 is designed to be scalable: requirements that are not relevant to the organisation’s activities can be excluded from scope with documented justification. A small organisation with a limited AI system portfolio and a narrow certification scope can implement and certify the standard with proportionate effort. The management system framework does not require a large dedicated team — it requires clear ownership, documented processes, and a commitment to review and improve.
