High-Risk AI Systems Under the EU AI Act

How a system gets classified as high-risk under Annex III or Annex I, the substantive Chapter III Section 2 obligations that follow, and the confirmed application dates now that the Digital Omnibus has moved them.

A conveyor belt of sealed shipping crates passing through a customs checkpoint gate, evoking the pre-market conformity gate that high-risk AI systems must clear before reaching the EU market.

    Requirements

    What organizations must do

    • Confirm the system is an "AI system" within Article 3(1) at all before doing anything else.
    • Run the Article 6 classification test against both the Annex I product-safety track and the Annex III use-case track.
    • If Annex III applies, check whether the narrow Article 6(3) derogation removes the system from high-risk status — remembering profiling always overrides the derogation.
    • For any system that remains high-risk, begin work against all six Chapter III Section 2 pillars together — they are cumulative, not alternative, requirements.
    • If relying on the Article 6(3) derogation, document that assessment before market placement — Article 6(4) makes this a legal requirement, not a best practice.
    • Start the classification exercise well ahead of the 2 December 2027 (Annex III) or 2 August 2028 (Annex I) deadlines — classification alone can be genuinely difficult at the margins.

    Common mistakes

    Treating the Annex I and Annex III tracks as mutually exclusive is a common error — a system embedded in Annex-I-covered machinery that is also used for one of the Annex III purposes can be caught by both tracks, each with its own conformity-assessment mechanics.

    Relying on the Article 6(3) derogation without documenting the assessment is a second common mistake — Article 6(4) makes the documentation itself a legal requirement, and an undocumented "not high-risk" call carries the same registration exposure as an undocumented high-risk one.

    Assuming the Article 6(3) derogation is available whenever a system merely "assists" a human decision is a third mistake worth flagging — the derogation's own text preserves high-risk status wherever the system performs profiling, and the "narrow procedural task" and "improves the result of a previously completed human activity" categories are narrower in practice than they may sound, since Commission guidance is intended to clarify exactly where that line sits.

    Open questions

    • Whether the Digital Omnibus changed the substance of Article 6's classification mechanics or the Annex I legislation list, beyond the dates. The AI Act Service Desk's own pages for Article 6 and Annex I carry the same Commission disclaimer seen in AR-000200 for Article 3: both provisions have "been amended by the Digital Omnibus on AI" and the displayed text "has not yet been updated to reflect those amendments." This article cites the pre-Omnibus text, cross-checked against the Omnibus's own confirmed effect on application dates — but whether the substance changed beyond the dates is unconfirmed.
    • Whether the Commission met its Article 6(5) statutory deadline (2 February 2026) for high-risk classification guidelines. Not independently confirmed against a primary Commission source on this research pass.
    • Where the practical boundary of the Article 6(3) derogation categories sits — particularly "narrow procedural task" and "improves the result of a previously completed human activity" — pending the Article 6(5) guidelines' worked examples.
