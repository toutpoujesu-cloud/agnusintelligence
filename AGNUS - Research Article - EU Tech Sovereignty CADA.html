European Union AI Governance · Sovereignty 10 September 2026

The European Commission proposes a Tech Sovereignty Package, including a Cloud and AI Development Act

The Commission's package includes the proposed Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA), Chips Act 2.0, an EU Open Source Strategy, and an energy digitalisation roadmap — aimed at strengthening European autonomy in AI infrastructure and digital sovereignty by 2030.

What happened

The European Commission has proposed a Technology Sovereignty Package, announced on 3 June 2026 and confirmed as a newly principal monitored item on the Commission's Digital Strategy homepage as of 10 September 2026. The package is a collection of legislative proposals and policy initiatives aimed at strengthening Europe's autonomous capacity in digital technologies — particularly AI and cloud infrastructure.

The centrepiece of the package that is most relevant to AI governance is the proposed Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA), which the Commission describes as targeting research and innovation, data-centre capacity, and EU autonomy in AI infrastructure. CADA has not yet been adopted — it is a Commission proposal subject to the ordinary legislative procedure.

The four components of the package

Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA)

Proposes a single EU-wide sovereignty assessment framework for cloud and AI, with public-sector adoption mechanisms and research and innovation incentives.

Chips Act 2.0

Updates the 2023 European Chips Act to address semiconductor supply chain resilience and EU capacity in advanced chip design and manufacturing.

EU Open Source Strategy

A cross-Commission strategy for the use, development, and governance of open-source software and AI within the EU public sector and research ecosystem.

Digitalisation and AI for energy

A roadmap for applying AI and digital technologies to energy system optimisation, grid management, and the EU's energy transition goals.

The Cloud and AI Development Act

CADA is the most significant element of the package for AI governance. Based on the Commission's description, the Act would introduce:

A single EU-wide sovereignty assessment framework

CADA proposes a unified framework for assessing cloud and AI sovereignty across EU member states — establishing common criteria for evaluating whether cloud and AI services meet European autonomy requirements, including data localisation, security, and operational control.

Public-sector adoption mechanism

The Act includes a mechanism for EU public-sector entities to adopt cloud and AI services that meet the sovereignty assessment criteria. This is potentially significant for AI vendors seeking to access EU government and public-sector markets, as it would create a structured certification or assessment route for qualifying services.

Research and innovation incentives

CADA includes provisions to incentivise European research and innovation in AI and cloud — likely through funding mechanisms, infrastructure access, and regulatory facilitation for European AI developers and cloud providers.

Legislative stage: CADA is a Commission proposal. It must pass through the European Parliament and the Council before it becomes EU law. This process typically takes 18 months to several years for complex legislation. The final text may differ substantially from the Commission's proposal. Organisations should monitor the legislative process rather than preparing for compliance with the proposed text.

Why this matters

The Tech Sovereignty Package — and CADA in particular — represents the Commission's attempt to address the structural challenge that European public and private sectors currently rely heavily on non-European cloud and AI infrastructure. The package acknowledges that EU sovereignty in AI is not just a regulatory question (addressed by the AI Act) but an infrastructure and capability question.

For AI vendors and cloud providers operating in Europe, the package signals a likely future in which EU public-sector procurement increasingly favours services that meet CADA sovereignty criteria. This is not a near-term compliance requirement, but it is a medium-term market-access consideration for organisations seeking to win EU public-sector AI contracts.

The package also interacts directly with the EU AI Act — CADA's sovereignty framework may complement the AI Act's provisions on GPAI model transparency and data governance, creating a layered EU regulatory environment for AI infrastructure.

What organisations should watch

  • European Parliament's position on CADA — particularly the sovereignty assessment criteria and public-sector mechanism
  • Council negotiations on CADA — member state positions on sovereignty requirements and procurement mechanisms
  • Whether CADA creates a formal cloud and AI "European sovereignty certification" or relies on assessment criteria applied case-by-case
  • How Chips Act 2.0 intersects with CADA on semiconductor supply chain requirements for AI hardware
  • The EU Open Source Strategy's implications for AI model governance and open-weight model deployment by public-sector entities
