AI Procurement Governance: What to Require from AI Vendors

Deploying a vendor's AI product does not transfer regulatory accountability. Under the EU AI Act, the deployer remains accountable for ensuring the system is used appropriately, that human oversight is in place, and that incidents are reported. This article maps what deployers should require from their AI vendors — in pre-procurement assessment, in contract terms, and as ongoing obligations.

Jean-Daniel Alexis· 11 September 2026· AI Governance pillar· AR-000610

The deployer liability problem

The EU AI Act distributes responsibility between providers (who build AI systems) and deployers (who put them into use). Providers bear the primary obligations for high-risk AI — conformity assessment, technical documentation, registration. But deployers are not passive recipients of a compliant product. Article 26 imposes direct obligations on deployers that cannot be contracted away to the provider.

Deployers must: use the system in accordance with its instructions for use; implement human oversight measures; monitor operation; report serious incidents; and, for systems that process personal data, conduct a DPIA where required. None of these obligations transfer to the vendor simply because the deployer did not build the system.

This means AI procurement is a governance-heavy process. The deployer must understand the system well enough to fulfil its own obligations — which requires the vendor to provide sufficient documentation, transparency, and support to make that possible.

Pre-procurement vendor assessment

Assessment areaQuestions to askRequirement level
EU AI Act classification Has the vendor assessed whether the system is high-risk under Annex III? If yes, has conformity assessment been completed? Is the system registered in the EU database? Must ask — non-compliance transfers risk to deployer
Technical documentation Is Annex IV technical documentation available? Does it include training data description, testing results, known limitations, and intended purpose? Must require for high-risk AI; should request for all AI systems
Instructions for use Are instructions for use available? Do they specify intended purpose, conditions of use, performance limitations, and human oversight requirements? Must require — deployer cannot fulfil Article 26 without them
Incident history Has the system been involved in any serious incidents? What is the vendor's incident response track record? Is there a public vulnerability disclosure policy? Should ask
Data processing What personal data does the system process? Where is it stored? What sub-processors are involved? Is a data processing agreement in place? Must establish — GDPR Article 28 requires a DPA with processors

Contractual requirements for AI procurement

AI procurement contracts should go beyond standard SaaS terms in several areas. The following provisions are relevant for deployers of AI systems that fall within EU AI Act scope or that process personal data:

Notification of material changes. The vendor must notify the deployer before making material changes to the model, training data, or system architecture that could affect the deployer's risk profile or compliance position. For high-risk AI, material changes may require a new conformity assessment.

Incident notification. The vendor must notify the deployer of serious incidents and near-misses within a defined timeframe. The deployer needs this information to fulfil its own incident reporting obligations under Article 73.

Audit rights. For high-risk AI, the deployer should retain the right to audit the vendor's compliance with its representations — or to require third-party audit evidence. This is particularly important for systems where the deployer's own regulator may request evidence of the vendor's compliance.

Termination for regulatory non-compliance. The contract should include a right for the deployer to terminate if the vendor's system fails to maintain compliance with applicable regulations, without penalty. A deployer should not be locked into a non-compliant vendor.

Data deletion and portability. On termination, the vendor must delete the deployer's data and, where applicable, provide it in a portable format. GDPR Article 20 data portability obligations may apply depending on the deployment context.

Ongoing vendor management obligations

Procurement governance does not end at contract signature. Deployers should maintain ongoing vendor oversight through four mechanisms:

Annual compliance review. Review the vendor's compliance position annually against the requirements at time of procurement. Check for changes in the vendor's EU AI Act classification, data processing practices, or sub-processor relationships.

Change notification tracking. Maintain a log of all material changes notified by the vendor and assess the governance implications of each change before it takes effect in the deployer's environment.

Incident log maintenance. Maintain a log of all incidents and near-misses involving the vendor's system, with assessment of whether they trigger the deployer's own reporting obligations.

Performance monitoring. Track the system's performance against the metrics established at procurement. Systematic performance degradation may indicate model drift, data poisoning, or a change in the vendor's service quality — all of which have governance implications.
