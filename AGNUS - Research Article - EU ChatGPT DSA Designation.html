The European Commission designates ChatGPT as a Very Large Online Search Engine under the Digital Services Act

OpenAI's ChatGPT has been formally designated as a VLOSE under the DSA, triggering systemic-risk obligations — including assessment and mitigation of risks involving algorithmic systems — that must be met by January 2027.

What happened

On 31 August 2026, the European Commission formally designated OpenAI's ChatGPT as a Very Large Online Search Engine (VLOSE) under the Digital Services Act (DSA). The Commission stated that ChatGPT had declared at least 45 million average monthly users in the European Union — the threshold at which VLOSE designation applies.

ChatGPT joins a small group of services previously designated as Very Large Online Platforms or Very Large Online Search Engines under the DSA, including Google Search, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and others. This is the first designation of a generative-AI service in this category.

The designation is significant because VLOSE status triggers a distinct and more demanding set of obligations under the DSA, separate from those that apply to smaller services. These obligations apply to the service, not to the model itself.

Key deadline: The Commission stated that the additional obligations must be met within four months of designation — by January 2027. This includes the systemic-risk assessment and mitigation obligations, which specifically cover risks involving algorithmic systems.

What the designation requires

The DSA imposes specific obligations on VLOSEs that do not apply to smaller platforms. For ChatGPT, the most relevant provisions cover three areas:

1. Systemic risk assessment

ChatGPT is now required to identify and assess systemic risks that its service — including its algorithmic systems — may generate within the EU. The DSA defines systemic risks to include: dissemination of illegal content, adverse effects on fundamental rights, gender-based violence, electoral integrity, and public security.

For a generative-AI service, the systemic-risk assessment obligation is novel territory. The Commission has not yet published specific guidance on how generative-AI systemic risks should be assessed under the DSA framework. ChatGPT will need to interpret and apply the existing regulatory text to its specific model architecture and user interaction patterns.

2. Risk mitigation measures

Following the risk assessment, ChatGPT must implement reasonable, proportionate measures to mitigate identified systemic risks. This could include changes to algorithmic recommendation systems, content moderation procedures, user safeguards for specific categories (minors, vulnerable users), or transparency measures.

3. Annual audit

VLOSEs are required to commission annual independent audits of compliance with their DSA obligations. These audits must be conducted by independent bodies and reported to the Commission. The first audit obligation runs from the date of designation.

4. Data access for researchers

VLOSEs must provide vetted researchers with access to data necessary to understand the systemic risks their services generate. The practical modalities for ChatGPT — a generative model, not a traditional search engine — remain to be defined in dialogue with the Commission.

Why this matters for AI governance

This designation sets a precedent: it confirms that the DSA's VLOSE category applies to generative-AI services with sufficient EU user volume, not only to traditional search engines and social platforms. This has direct implications for other large AI services — including competitors to ChatGPT — that have declared or are approaching 45 million EU monthly active users.

The interaction between the DSA designation and the EU AI Act creates a dual regulatory framework for ChatGPT in the EU. The AI Act's general-purpose AI provisions apply to the underlying model; the DSA's VLOSE obligations apply to the user-facing service. Compliance teams will need to address both frameworks simultaneously.
