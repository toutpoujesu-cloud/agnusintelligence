Italy AI Governance: Garante, AI Act Enforcement and the Italian AI Strategy

Italy became internationally significant in AI governance in March 2023 when the Garante — Italy's Data Protection Authority — issued a temporary ban on ChatGPT, becoming the first European DPA to take such action against a major AI service. The ban, lifted after OpenAI addressed the Garante's concerns, demonstrated that GDPR can be used as an enforcement tool against AI services — even before the EU AI Act applies.

The Garante: Italy's proactive DPA

The Garante per la Protezione dei Dati Personali — Italy's independent data protection authority — has been one of the most active European DPAs in applying GDPR to AI systems. Unlike some European DPAs that have taken a cautious, observe-and-wait approach to AI enforcement, the Garante has repeatedly used its existing GDPR powers to investigate and take action against AI services.

The Garante's approach is grounded in GDPR's existing provisions — particularly the lawful basis requirement, the right to erasure, the obligation to provide privacy information, the prohibition on automated decision-making with significant effects, and the age verification requirements. The Garante treats these as applying fully and immediately to AI services — not as requiring new AI-specific regulation before enforcement is possible.

This approach has made the Garante a leading indicator of how GDPR enforcement will develop across Europe as AI systems become more prevalent. DPAs in other member states — France's CNIL, the Irish DPC, Germany's BfDI — have referenced the Garante's ChatGPT investigation and its conclusions in their own AI enforcement work.

The ChatGPT case and its lessons

In March 2023, the Garante issued an emergency ban on ChatGPT's processing of Italian users' personal data. The Garante's concerns centred on: (1) no age verification to prevent minors from using the service; (2) no lawful basis for processing personal data to train ChatGPT's underlying model; (3) inaccurate information generated about real individuals; (4) no mechanism for Italian users to exercise data subject rights including rectification and erasure.

OpenAI addressed the Garante's concerns over the following weeks: implementing an age gate for Italian users; clarifying the legal basis for data processing; providing a mechanism for Italian users to opt out of their data being used for model training; and establishing a process for individuals to request correction or removal of information about themselves.

The case established several principles that have influenced European AI enforcement more broadly: GDPR applies to AI training data even when users voluntarily submit data through an interface; inaccurate outputs about real people may constitute a GDPR violation (inaccurate data); and services without adequate age verification violate the obligation to protect minors' personal data.

Italian AI Strategy 2024–2026

Italy's National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2024–2026, adopted in December 2023, identifies AI as a strategic priority for Italian economic competitiveness and public administration modernisation. The Strategy identifies seven priority sectors: PA (public administration), healthcare, agri-food, manufacturing, energy and environment, culture and tourism, and financial services.

Italy is also the designated national authority for the AI Office under the EU AI Act — meaning Italian public sector AI deployments will be among the first to operate under the Act's deployer obligations framework. The Agency for Digital Italy (AGID) and the National Cybersecurity Agency (ACN) have roles in the Italian AI governance ecosystem alongside the Garante.

For operators in Italy

The Garante is an active enforcement risk. Organisations deploying AI that processes personal data of Italian users face a higher probability of Garante inquiry than in many other European markets. The Garante's track record on ChatGPT and subsequent AI investigations suggests it will continue proactive enforcement. Data protection impact assessments and documented lawful bases for AI data processing are particularly important for the Italian market.

EU AI Act direct application. As an EU member state, Italy will apply the EU AI Act directly when its provisions take effect. Italy will have national market surveillance authorities designated under the Act — organisations operating in Italy will interact with Italian authorities for EU AI Act compliance from 2026.

Public procurement opportunities. Italy's AI Strategy identifies substantial public investment in AI for public administration. Organisations providing AI to Italian public authorities will face the EU AI Act's Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment requirements (Article 27) for high-impact systems in public sector contexts from 2026.
