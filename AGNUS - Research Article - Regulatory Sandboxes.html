AI Regulatory Sandboxes Under the EU AI Act

Article 57 requires Member States to establish at least one AI regulatory sandbox by 2 August 2026. Sandboxes are supervised environments where providers can develop, train, test and validate AI systems before market placement — with reduced regulatory friction, SME priority access, a 12-month duration extendable once, and explicit protection from administrative fines for good-faith participants.

Key context
"Regulatory sandboxes create a supervised space where innovators can test AI systems before they are fully governed. Access is not a right — it must be applied for and approved."
EU AI Act, Article 57
A sandbox is not a deregulation mechanism — it is a supervised testing channel. Participants operate under a sandbox plan, are monitored by competent authorities, and may lose access if conditions are not met.

    Executive summary

    What a sandbox is

    The three core provisions

    Duration
    12 months
    Extendable once for a further 12 months
    The 24-month maximum is a hard ceiling. Participants who need more time must exit and re-enter through normal market pathways or apply under a new plan.
    Access priority
    SMEs first
    Start-ups and SMEs have explicit priority access
    Competent authorities must take active steps to make sandboxes accessible. This includes proportionate application requirements and, where available, dedicated SME support within the sandbox.
    Fine protection
    Good-faith shield
    Violations found during sandbox do not automatically trigger fines
    Applies where the provider acted in good faith, followed competent authority guidance, and took corrective action when directed. The shield does not cover deliberate non-compliance or failure to follow the sandbox plan.

    Requirements and obligations

    What organizations must do

    • Identify the sandbox operated by the relevant national competent authority in each Member State where you develop or plan to deploy AI systems — at least one must be operational by 2 August 2026.
    • Assess whether your AI system is a candidate for sandbox participation: systems in development, with uncertain classification, or with novel use cases in regulated sectors are the strongest candidates.
    • Prepare a sandbox plan before applying: the plan must describe the system, its intended purpose, the testing methodology, the safeguards for affected persons, and the success metrics.
    • Identify any sector-specific authorities that must be involved in the sandbox for your use case (financial, healthcare, critical infrastructure) and factor their requirements into the sandbox plan.
    • Document all guidance received from competent authorities during the sandbox period and all corrective actions taken — this documentation is the basis for the fine protection if a violation is discovered.
    • Plan the post-sandbox compliance pathway before entering: the sandbox does not replace conformity assessment, technical documentation, registration or other Article 16 obligations at market entry.
    • If processing personal data in the sandbox, review Article 60 conditions and reflect them in the sandbox plan — Article 60 is not a GDPR exemption but a supervised-testing channel with its own requirements.

    Common mistakes

    Treating the sandbox as a deregulation mechanism is a common mistake — Article 57 is a supervised testing channel, not a carve-out from the Act’s requirements. Participants are monitored, follow a plan, and must still complete full conformity assessment on exit.

    Assuming fine protection is automatic is a second mistake — the good-faith shield applies where the provider followed competent authority guidance and took corrective action. Ignoring guidance or diverging from the sandbox plan removes the protection.

    Treating sandbox participation as a substitute for conformity assessment is a third mistake — the sandbox is a development and testing phase; conformity assessment, technical documentation, and registration are still required before market entry.

    Assuming Article 60 exempts data processing from GDPR is a fourth mistake — Article 60 is a specific supervised-testing regime with conditions, not a general data protection derogation.

    Open questions

    • Whether the Digital Omnibus changed Article 57’s sandbox framework — Article 57 carries the Omnibus disclaimer; the provisions described here are pre-amendment.
    • Which Member States have established operational sandboxes by 2 August 2026 and what their application procedures look like — not surveyed on this research pass; national implementations differ materially.
    • Whether the AI Office has established a Union-level sandbox and what access conditions it sets — Article 57(2) permits but does not mandate a Union-level sandbox; status as of research date is not confirmed.
    • How the good-faith fine protection interacts with Article 99’s proportionality factors — Article 99(7) lists cooperation and self-reporting as factors; whether sandbox participation and guidance-following are independently treated as mitigating factors is not elaborated in the statute.

    Frequently asked

    FAQ
    Is sandbox participation mandatory for high-risk AI providers?
    No. Article 57 creates an optional testing pathway. Providers may proceed directly to conformity assessment, technical documentation, and registration without ever using a sandbox. The sandbox is most valuable for systems where regulatory classification is uncertain, where the use case is novel, or where the provider wants regulatory guidance before committing to a specific compliance architecture.
    FAQ
    Does sandbox participation mean the AI system is approved for market placement?
    No. Exiting the sandbox means the development and testing phase is complete under regulatory supervision. The provider still needs to complete conformity assessment (Article 43), finalise technical documentation (Articles 11 and Annex IV), and register the system (Article 49) before placing it on the market. Sandbox exit is a milestone in the compliance pathway, not the end of it.
    FAQ
    Can a provider enter the same sandbox twice for the same system?
    Article 57 allows a 12-month period extendable once for a further 12 months — a maximum of 24 months per system. The statute does not explicitly address re-entry after a completed or terminated sandbox; this would depend on national competent authority rules. Substantially modified systems may be treated as new systems for sandbox purposes.
    FAQ
    What is the relationship between the EU AI Act sandbox and existing national innovation sandboxes?
    Some Member States already operate financial services, data protection, or sector-specific innovation sandboxes. Article 57(1) requires the AI Act sandbox to be operational by 2 August 2026 — this may be a new standalone sandbox or an extension of an existing one. The key requirement is that the AI Act competent authority is involved in oversight.
