Registration Requirements for High-Risk AI Systems

Before a high-risk AI system can be placed on the market, the provider must register both themselves and the system in the EU database. Three registration channels exist depending on the Annex III category: the general EU database, a restricted non-public section for sensitive use-cases, and national-level registration for critical infrastructure. Providers claiming the Article 6(3) derogation must also register.

Key context
"Registration in the EU database is a precondition to placing a high-risk AI system on the market — not a post-market formality."
EU AI Act, Article 49
Registration is a pre-market gate, not a post-market formality — the provider and system must be in the EU database before placing on the market, not after.
Contents

    Builds on AR-000163 (Risk Classification) and AR-000240 (Provider Obligations) — none of their content is re-derived here. Article 49 is the registration gate that follows conformity assessment (AR-000350) and precedes placing on the market. Article 71 establishes and governs the EU database that Article 49 references.

    Executive summary

    The three registration channels

    Article 49(1)
    General EU database
    Annex III points 3, 4, 5, 8
    Education, employment, essential services, administration of justice. Publicly accessible. Provider and system both registered before market placement.
    Article 49(4)
    Secure non-public section
    Annex III points 1, 6, 7
    Biometrics, law enforcement, migration/asylum/border control. Accessible to Commission and national authorities only. Same EU database, restricted access.
    Article 49(5)
    National-level registration
    Annex III point 2
    Critical infrastructure AI systems. Registered at Member State level rather than in the EU database. Mechanics of national registration not specified in Article 49 itself.

    Requirements

    What organizations must do

    • Determine the registration channel before beginning the registration process — Annex III point 2 (critical infrastructure) registers nationally; points 1, 6, 7 (biometrics, law enforcement, migration) go to the restricted EU database section; all other Annex III points go to the general EU database.
    • Register both the provider (or authorised representative) and the AI system — Article 49(1) requires both registrations.
    • Complete registration before placing on the market or putting into service — not concurrently, not after.
    • If claiming the Article 6(3) derogation: register under Article 49(2) even though the full high-risk obligation set does not apply — the derogation does not waive registration.
    • For Annex III point 2 critical infrastructure systems: determine which Member State’s national registration mechanism applies and complete national registration accordingly.

    Common mistakes

    Treating registration as a post-market administrative step rather than a pre-market gate is a common mistake — Article 49(1) requires registration before placing on the market, and a system placed without prior registration is non-compliant from day one.

    Assuming the Article 6(3) derogation waives registration is a second mistake — Article 49(2) explicitly requires registration even when the provider concludes the system is not high-risk under the derogation.

    Registering only the system without registering the provider (or vice versa) is a third mistake — Article 49(1) requires registration of both.

    Registering a critical infrastructure system (Annex III point 2) in the EU database rather than at national level is a fourth mistake — Article 49(5) explicitly routes these to national-level registration, excepting them from the EU database requirement in Article 49(1).

    Open questions

    • Whether the Digital Omnibus changed Article 49’s registration channels or the derogation-registration rule — Article 49’s Service Desk page was confirmed clean (no Omnibus disclaimer) during the AR-000240 research pass. This is treated as consistent with no amendment, not confirmed as unamended.
    • What national registration mechanisms Member States have established for Annex III point 2 systems — not surveyed; each Member State sets its own mechanism, and they are not listed in Article 49 itself.
    • Whether the EU database (Article 71) is fully operational and accepting registrations as of this research pass — not confirmed against a primary source.

    Frequently asked

    FAQ
    Must a provider who claims the Article 6(3) derogation also register?
    Yes. Article 49(2) explicitly requires such a provider to register both itself and the system in the EU database. The derogation exempts the provider from the Chapter III Section 2 compliance obligations but not from registration.
    FAQ
    Which Annex III systems go into the restricted non-public section of the EU database?
    Points 1 (biometrics), 6 (law enforcement), and 7 (migration/asylum/border control). These are accessible only to the Commission and national competent authorities, not to the public.
    FAQ
    Does an authorised representative register on behalf of a third-country provider, or does the provider register directly?
    Article 49(1) requires registration by “the provider or, where applicable, the authorised representative.” Where a provider has appointed an authorised representative (required for third-country providers under Article 22), the authorised representative performs the registration.
