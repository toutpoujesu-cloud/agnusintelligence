South Korea AI Governance: AI Framework Act, PIPC and the K-AI Strategy

South Korea enacted the Act on the Development of Artificial Intelligence and Establishment of Trust (AI Framework Act) in October 2024 — one of the first comprehensive national AI laws enacted outside the EU. Combined with an active Personal Information Protection Commission and a national AI strategy targeting global leadership in AI development, South Korea is rapidly establishing itself as a significant AI governance actor in Asia.

The AI Framework Act

The Act on the Development of Artificial Intelligence and Establishment of Trust — commonly called the AI Framework Act — was enacted by the National Assembly in October 2024. It is a framework law: it establishes principles, governance structures, and a national AI policy framework, delegating specific regulatory requirements to implementing legislation and sector-specific regulations.

The AI Framework Act establishes: the National AI Committee (chaired by the Prime Minister) as the top-level AI governance body; obligations for the government to establish national AI strategies; the concept of "high-impact AI" (analogous to EU AI Act high-risk AI) as the category of AI requiring the most stringent governance; rights for individuals affected by AI systems including the right to explanation and contestation; and a framework for AI sandboxes to enable innovation while managing risk.

The AI Framework Act's approach to "high-impact AI" is less prescriptive than the EU AI Act's Annex III list — it designates AI used in areas with "significant impact on human life, safety, and fundamental rights" as high-impact, with the specific list to be determined by ministerial decree. As of September 2026, the implementing regulations determining the specific scope of high-impact AI had not been published.

PIPC and AI: PIPA amendments for AI data

The Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC) — South Korea's data protection authority — has been actively developing guidance on AI and data governance. Most significantly, in August 2026, the PIPC published proposed amendments to the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) that would create specific legal provisions for AI processing of personal data.

The proposed PIPA amendments would: create a new legal basis for AI training on publicly available personal data (subject to conditions); establish requirements for transparency about AI-generated content; introduce specific provisions for automated decision-making affecting individuals; and require data protection impact assessments for high-risk AI processing. These amendments directly address the legal uncertainty around AI training data under the current PIPA framework — which, like GDPR, did not anticipate AI training at scale.

The PIPC amendments were at consultation stage as of September 2026. Their enactment would make South Korea's AI data governance framework significantly more specific and comprehensive than PIPA's current general provisions — moving South Korea closer to the EU's dual AI Act + GDPR structure.

The K-AI Strategy: global leadership ambition

South Korea's K-AI (Korean AI) Strategy, announced in 2024, targets South Korea becoming one of the three leading AI nations globally by 2030. The strategy identifies specific industrial sectors — semiconductors, displays, automotive, healthcare, manufacturing, and financial services — where AI leadership is targeted. Substantial public investment in AI infrastructure, talent, and R&D is committed under the strategy.

South Korea's industrial AI strategy interacts with its governance strategy in an interesting way: the K-AI Strategy creates urgency to maintain competitive AI capabilities, which creates pressure against over-regulation. The AI Framework Act's framework approach reflects this — setting governance principles without prescriptive rules that might constrain AI development.

EU adequacy and cross-border considerations

South Korea received an EU adequacy decision in December 2021 — the first such decision in Asia alongside Japan. This enables personal data transfers between the EU and South Korea without additional safeguards, facilitating cross-border AI development and deployment involving both jurisdictions.

For organisations developing AI in South Korea for EU deployment, or vice versa, the adequacy decision significantly simplifies data governance for training datasets and inference pipelines. However, the substantive requirements of EU GDPR (particularly automated decision-making protections under Article 22) and South Korea's PIPA remain different — EU-destined AI systems must comply with EU GDPR regardless of where development occurred.
