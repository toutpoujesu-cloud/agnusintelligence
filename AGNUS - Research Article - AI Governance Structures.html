AI Governance Structures: Boards, Committees and Accountable Officers

AI governance without a clear governance structure is a policy document without an owner. This article maps the organisational structures organisations are using to govern AI — from board-level AI committees to Chief AI Officers to operational governance teams — and explains what accountability chain each structure creates.

Jean-Daniel Alexis· 11 September 2026· AI Governance pillar· AR-000590

Why structure matters

The most common failure mode in enterprise AI governance is not bad policy — it is policy that belongs to no one. Organisations write AI principles, publish AI ethics frameworks, and adopt AI risk taxonomies. But when a decision must be made about whether a specific deployment is acceptable, or when an incident occurs, there is no clear owner of the decision, no clear escalation path, and no clear accountability for the outcome.

Structure creates accountability. It determines who makes decisions, who is accountable for outcomes, who is informed, and who has the authority to stop a deployment. Without a structure, governance is advisory at best and decorative at worst.

The EU AI Act creates external pressure toward formal governance structure. Article 26 (obligations of deployers) and Article 72 (post-market monitoring) both require organisational processes — not just policies — for AI governance. Supervisory authorities will look for evidence that governance is operational, not aspirational.

Three organisational models

Model 1
Centralised — AI Centre of Excellence
A dedicated team owns AI governance across the organisation. Business units request approvals and receive guidance from the central team. Advantage: consistency and expertise. Risk: bottleneck, disconnection from operational context, and a tendency to become a compliance function rather than an enablement function.
Model 2
Federated — AI Champions Model
Each business unit has a designated AI governance lead (an "AI Champion") who is responsible for governance in their domain. A central team provides standards, tools, and oversight but not operational authority. Advantage: closer to context. Risk: inconsistency and the "AI Champion" role being underfunded and undertrained.
Model 3
Hybrid — Governance Board with Operational Leads
A cross-functional AI Governance Board sets policy and handles escalations. Operational governance is delegated to domain leads. The board meets regularly to review deployments, approve high-risk systems, and handle incidents. Most common in regulated industries. Most consistent with EU AI Act requirements.

What regulation requires from governance structures

The EU AI Act does not prescribe a specific organisational structure. It prescribes outcomes: that high-risk AI systems are assessed before deployment, monitored during operation, and that incidents are reported. The structure that achieves those outcomes is left to the organisation to design.

However, the Act's requirements are operational enough to imply a structure. Article 26 (deployer obligations) requires deployers to monitor operation, take action when risks materialise, and report serious incidents. Article 14 requires that natural persons can oversee and intervene in AI system operation. These obligations require people with defined responsibilities — not just policies.

The NIST AI RMF's GOVERN function maps to governance structure most directly. Its GOVERN 1.1 action requires that organisational policies and processes exist for AI risk management — and that they are implemented and documented. The AI RMF Playbook specifies that governance structures should include clear roles, responsibilities, and accountabilities — not just for the AI function, but for the board level and operational level alike.

For financial services organisations, EBA and ESMA guidance on AI adds sector-specific requirements. The EBA's guidelines on internal governance require that AI systems used in credit risk assessment are subject to the same senior management accountability as other material risk management systems.
