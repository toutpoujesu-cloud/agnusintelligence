AR-000400 South Korea
South Korea AI Data Governance 22 August 2026

South Korea amends the Personal Information Protection Act to create a dedicated AI development data route

The National Assembly has passed an amendment to the PIPA that establishes a conditional legal basis for using personal data to train AI systems. Promulgation, subordinate legislation, and an uncertain effective date follow.

What happened

South Korea's National Assembly passed an amendment to the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) — the country's primary personal-data law — that creates a new, conditional legal basis permitting the use of personal data for AI model development and training.

The amendment was passed at a National Assembly plenary session and announced by the Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC), the independent supervisory authority responsible for enforcing Korean data-protection law. This is a legislative development, not an executive or regulatory action.

As of the date of this record (22 August 2026), the amendment had passed the National Assembly but had not yet been promulgated. Promulgation by the President is required for the amendment to enter into legal force. The effective date and any subordinate legislation (presidential decrees, ministerial rules) had not been published at the time of this writing.

Editorial caution: The passage of the amendment at the National Assembly plenary session is not equivalent to promulgation or current legal effectiveness. Readers should not treat this article as a statement that the law is currently in force. Track promulgation and subordinate legislation before treating the new data route as available for use.

What the amendment provides

The PIPA amendment creates what the PIPC describes as a route for "safe AI development" using personal data. Specifically, it establishes a conditional legal basis — separate from the existing consent-based grounds — that permits personal data to be used for the development and training of AI systems, subject to approval criteria and safeguards that will be defined in subordinate legislation.

The key elements of the new route, based on what is publicly known at this stage, are:

  • Conditional approval: organisations wishing to use personal data for AI training under the new route must meet criteria to be established in subordinate legislation, not in the Act itself.
  • Safeguard requirements: the amendment anticipates that implementing regulations will specify the safeguards that must be in place — including, likely, data minimisation, purpose limitation, and security controls — before the route can be used.
  • PIPC oversight: the Personal Information Protection Commission retains supervisory authority. Use of the new route does not remove data subjects' existing rights under the PIPA.
  • Implementation timetable: the operational requirements, approval criteria, and effective date will follow promulgation and the publication of presidential decree and ministerial rules — none of which had been published at the time of this record.

Why this matters

South Korea's PIPA is one of the most comprehensive personal-data frameworks in Asia-Pacific and has historically been interpreted strictly on consent requirements. The amendment is significant because it signals that Korea is moving towards a purpose-specific legal basis for AI training data — a model that mirrors, without copying, the EU AI Act's approach to data governance for high-risk AI systems.

For organisations operating in Korea, or processing data about Korean residents, the amendment signals a likely change in the legal landscape for AI training data. However, the operational requirements remain undefined until subordinate legislation is published. Acting on the assumption that the new route is currently available would be premature.

The amendment also has international relevance: it is part of a broader trend — visible in the EU, Japan, and Singapore — of regulators creating purpose-specific data routes for AI development outside the standard consent framework, while retaining oversight and subject rights.

What organisations should watch

  • The promulgation announcement from the Office of the President of Korea
  • Presidential decree and ministerial rules specifying approval criteria and safeguards
  • PIPC guidance on what constitutes a "safe AI development" use case under the amendment
  • The effective date — which may be phased, with some provisions applying sooner than others
  • Any parallel changes to the PIPC's complaint and enforcement processes that accompany the amendment
