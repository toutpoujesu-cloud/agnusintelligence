EU AI Act — What Changes for Banks After the GPAI Chapter Takes Full Effect

The General-Purpose AI chapter of the EU AI Act came into full effect in August 2025. For most banks, the immediate response was to assess whether any of their AI systems qualified as GPAI models — and most concluded that they did not, because they do not develop general-purpose AI. This conclusion was correct but incomplete.

Banks as GPAI deployers, not GPAI developers

The GPAI chapter creates obligations for two categories of actor: GPAI providers — the organisations that develop and supply GPAI models — and GPAI deployers — the organisations that integrate GPAI model outputs into their own products and services. Most banks fall into the second category.

A bank that uses an external large language model to generate draft regulatory reports, summarise loan applications, or respond to customer queries is integrating GPAI model outputs into a business process. Under Article 53, this makes it a GPAI deployer with specific obligations regarding evaluation, monitoring, and transparency. [SRC-0001]

The documentation requirements that are new

GPAI deployers in financial services must maintain technical documentation sufficient to demonstrate that they have assessed the AI system's fitness for purpose in their specific use case. For a bank using a third-party LLM, this means: records of the evaluation conducted before deployment; evidence of ongoing performance monitoring; documentation of any fine-tuning or system prompting applied; and records of user-reported issues.
