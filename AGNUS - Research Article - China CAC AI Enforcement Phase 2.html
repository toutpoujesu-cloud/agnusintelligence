China's CAC launches second phase of AI application enforcement, reporting 5.61 million removals

The Cyberspace Administration of China has announced a second phase of its Clear and Bright campaign targeting AI applications — covering false information, harmful content, impersonation, and risks involving minors. Cumulative enforcement statistics reveal the scale of operational exposure.

Source: Cyberspace Administration of China — official enforcement announcement (SRC-0101). Statistics are cumulative figures reported by CAC. AGNUS does not provide legal advice. Organisations with China operations should consult qualified local counsel on CAC compliance obligations.
Contents

    What happened

    On 2 September 2026, the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) announced the second phase of the Clear and Bright (朗朗乾坤) campaign targeting disorder in AI applications. The campaign focuses on content and behaviour issues arising from AI-generated material on platforms operating in China.

    The announcement included cumulative enforcement statistics from the first phase of the campaign, providing a clearer picture of the scale and nature of CAC's AI content enforcement in practice.

    What the second phase covers

    The second phase of the Clear and Bright campaign targets four categories of non-compliant AI application behaviour:

    • AI-generated false information: content generated or amplified by AI systems that makes false factual claims or misleads users
    • Harmful AI-generated content: content that violates Chinese content regulations, including material that undermines social stability, public morality, or national security
    • AI impersonation: use of AI to impersonate real persons — including public figures, officials, and media organisations — without authorisation
    • Risks involving minors: AI content or interaction patterns that pose risks to minors, including age-inappropriate content and data-handling practices

    Operational significance

    The cumulative statistics are significant for two reasons. First, they confirm that CAC is conducting large-scale active enforcement — not merely issuing guidelines — against AI content non-compliance. Platforms operating under CAC jurisdiction face real enforcement risk, not theoretical regulatory exposure.

    Second, the scale of removal actions (5.61 million items) and account actions (49,000) indicates that CAC has developed significant operational capacity for AI content enforcement. The CAC operates under the AI-generated content regulations (深度合成管理规定) and the generative AI measures (生成式人工智能服务管理暂行办法), which together require registration, content moderation, labelling, and reporting obligations for AI service providers in China.

    Exposure for international operators

    Organisations providing AI services that are accessible in China — whether through direct operation or through partners — face exposure under the CAC's enforcement framework. The second phase announcement signals that enforcement attention remains active and that the four categories identified are priority areas for inspection and action.

    Foreign companies that provide AI services to Chinese business partners, or whose AI outputs are distributed in China through third parties, should assess whether those arrangements create exposure under the deep synthesis regulations and the generative AI measures.

    What organisations should watch

    • CAC enforcement announcements following the second phase — including statistics, named platforms, and penalty disclosures
    • Updates to the AI-generated content labelling requirements, which remain an active area of CAC guidance
    • Any extension of the campaign's scope to cover cross-border AI service provision
    • The interaction between this enforcement campaign and China's developing AI standards, including national AI security standards under TC260
